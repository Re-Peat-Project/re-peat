# 1 Executive summary

The 2026 dry season made peat fire a national priority again. KLH/BPLH puts the burned area from January to October 2026 at about 1.64 million ha, about 673,279 ha of it on peat, and has set a restoration target of about 3.3 million ha in 337 priority KHG (KLH/BPLH 2026). The regulatory target is a water table no more than 0.4 m below the peat surface (PP 57/2016).

RE-PEAT is the peatland rewetting toolkit developed for the World Bank and KLH peatland rewetting project by its GIS workstream at the Department of Geodetic Engineering, Universitas Gadjah Mada. It works on the *KHG* (*Kesatuan Hidrologis Gambut*, peat hydrological unit), the official planning unit, and takes a KHG through five steps: terrain, sub-KHG units, water balance (*neraca air*), prioritisation, and the design of canal blocks and pumping (RE-PEAT Interventions). The same method applies to any of the 865 KHG. This report sets out the data, the methods and the evidence behind them, and the results to October 2026.

## Main findings

- Terrain data limit how precisely blocks can be planned. At two KHG with airborne LiDAR, five elevation models were benchmarked against the LiDAR, and the best of them, DeltaDTM after correction, errs by 0.47 to 0.64 m in height differences over 1 km, which is more than the real relief of the domes (0.38 to 0.46 m). DEMNAS, FABDEM and GLO-30 err by 1.7 to 2.5 m after correction. Because no model met the accuracy criterion set in advance, the whole chain runs once per elevation model and the spread between the runs is reported with the results.
- The mapped canal network, more than the terrain, sets the sub-KHG units. With primary and secondary canals and rivers as outlets, the 14 analysed KHG split into 22 to 30 units per analysis, and the layout agrees across terrain sources about as well as across noisy copies of the LiDAR.
- The national ranking picks out the KHG that burn later. All 865 KHG were profiled and 476 ranked on fire on peat, this year's hotspots and canals by class. With indicators from 2015 to 2022, the ranking separates the KHG that had hotspots in 2023 to 2025 with an AUC of 0.956, and ten of the 11 resolved main KHG fall in its top third. The ranking uses default weights proposed by the project for agreement with KLH and the World Bank, and the weights can be adjusted in the dashboard.
- In concessions, water management holds the water table up. At Sugihan-Lumpur the climate removed 307 mm in the 2026 dry season while the station water table fell only 11 cm, so about 290 mm, or 1.8 billion m³, came from outside the rain balance. The deficit was 211 mm at Air Hitam Laut-Buntu Kecil, 119 mm at Mendahara-Batanghari and 7 mm at Kampar-Gaung. It was 348 mm at Kahayan-Sebangau (one station only, so low confidence), 299 mm at Kapuas-Barito and 132 mm at Pungurbesar-Kapuas, and 0 mm at three Riau KHG where rain exceeded evapotranspiration; four KHG have no station inside them.
- Open climate data give each KHG a reliable calendar of dry months, but they separate sub-KHG reliably in only 2 of 12 runs at the four KHG where this was tested.
- Canal blocks raise the water table only near the blocked canal. A groundwater model for rewetting scenarios, which is in development, reproduces the water supply of managed land when that land is held at 0.4 m. At Mendahara-Batanghari, blocks cut the number of days with the water table deeper than 40 cm by 26 days on average within 150 m of a blocked reach, and by almost nothing beyond 350 m.
- Land outside licences can so far be tested against 6 community stations in one KHG. Once logger outliers are screened out, the model on a 100 m grid matches their level and fall when canals are set at the levels measured in LiDAR, and does not match them with canals at 1.2 m.
- The number of blocks depends on the terrain model. The blocks needed by the slope rule differ by a factor of 2.6 to 7.0 between elevation models at the five KHG with LiDAR, and DEMNAS gives 1.6 to 4.2 times the DeltaDTM count at the nine KHG without it. RE-PEAT Interventions keeps the blocks that give 90 % of the canal rewetting and applies the 500 m minimum spacing of the KLH guide, which gives 5 to 117 blocks per run. Budgets range from 1 to 620 billion Rp per run, driven by revegetation of burned land and, where the ranking counts many blocks, by the KLH estimate of missing blocks, and use proposed unit prices that are still to be verified; a KLH cost standard for rewetting works, once available, would replace them.

## Status

The full chain has been applied to 14 KHG: eleven of the 12 main KHG, two reserve KHG and the earlier test site Kampar-Gaung. Five of them (Air Hitam Laut-Buntu Kecil, Kampar-Gaung, Mendahara-Batanghari, Saleh-Sugihan and Sugihan-Lumpur) have three elevation models each, and nine have corrected DeltaDTM and DEMNAS only, which makes 33 analyses. The twelfth main KHG, Cawang-Lalang, is to be confirmed with KLH. Chapter 10 reports the results KHG by KHG. The national dashboard, the interventions report and print layouts for each KHG, and a QGIS plugin that guides KLH staff through the design stage by stage are in working order.

## Decisions and data requested

KLH and the World Bank are asked to agree the ranking weights, confirm the twelfth KHG, decide whether data under non-commercial licences may be used, and choose how the groundwater model represents water management in concessions (chapter 11). The results would gain most from water-table stations in land outside licences, measured canal water levels by season, LiDAR for the KHG where blocks will be designed, and a documented source for the burned-area layer.
