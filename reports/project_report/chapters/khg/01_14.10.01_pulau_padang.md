<!-- Writing prompt: Where the KHG lies, how much of it is peat, how it ranks nationally and why, what canal network and blocks it has, and which elevation models and steps have run. -->

Pulau Padang is a *KHG* (*Kesatuan Hidrologis Gambut*, peat hydrological unit) in Riau. It covers 1,112 km², of which 1,102 km² is peat, and ranks 194 of 476 ranked KHG nationally (medium priority) with the default weights proposed for agreement with KLH and the World Bank. Its burned share of peat is below 0.4 % a year and it stays between rank 171 and 269 under every weight preset (chapter 6). The mapped network has 121 km of primary, 188 km of secondary and 859 km of tertiary canals, and the national layer records 292 water-management structures, 156 of them realised canal blocks. Only 3 SiTMAT water-table stations lie inside the KHG. The 2 m LiDAR flown in 2016 is left out of the chain (chapter 4), so the KHG was analysed on corrected DeltaDTM and corrected DEMNAS, with no reference against which to measure their error. Every step has run on both models.

### Terrain and the three elevation models

{{figure: khg_dem_maps:14.10.01 | Pulau Padang: ground elevation from each elevation model on one colour scale, and their differences. Source: LiDAR terrain model, DeltaDTM and DEMNAS after the DEM correction | made by khg_dem_maps in re-peat/tools/project_report_khg.py from the KHG work folder}}

<!-- Writing prompt: Describe the terrain (dome, river levees, coast) and where the models disagree, for example along rivers, plantations or forest edges, and the likely reason (canopy left in the surface, coarse resolution, datum). -->

Both models show a raised centre with lower ground towards the eastern margin and the coast, and DeltaDTM draws it as a smooth surface. DEMNAS follows the same pattern but sits higher over most of the centre, west and south-east, and lower in a north-eastern strip; it is more than 2 m above DeltaDTM over 33 % of the KHG and more than 2 m below it over 11 %. A straight edge across the northern third, with a patchy block above it, looks like a tile boundary in DEMNAS rather than terrain. The likely causes are canopy and plantation cover left in the DEMNAS surface after the correction and its finer 8 m grid, which carries local noise that the 30 m DeltaDTM averages out.

{{table: khg_dem_stats:14.10.01 | Pulau Padang: resolution, cover and relief of each elevation model, and its difference to LiDAR. Source: LiDAR terrain model, DeltaDTM and DEMNAS after the DEM correction | made by khg_dem_stats in re-peat/tools/project_report_khg.py from the KHG work folder}}

<!-- Writing prompt: Compare relief and the median and spread of the difference to LiDAR; say whether the difference is a constant offset (datum) or local error, and what that means for the slopes the later steps use. -->

Relief between the 5th and 95th percentiles is 2.7 to 11.7 m on DeltaDTM (9.0 m) and 2.0 to 14.6 m on DEMNAS (12.6 m); both cover at least 99 % of the KHG. Without LiDAR no difference to a reference can be given, and the distribution of DeltaDTM minus DEMNAS has a 5th to 95th percentile of -5.0 to +3.1 m and a histogram peak at -0.75 m. A spread of that size is local error rather than a constant offset, so the slopes along canals will differ between the models. On the 2016 LiDAR of this KHG, smoothed DEMNAS had a height-difference error of 1.67 m over 1 km (chapter 4), well above the 0.3 m head drop between blocks.

### Sub-KHG units

{{figure: khg_units_maps:14.10.01 | Pulau Padang: sub-KHG units delineated on each elevation model. Source: sub-KHG delineation on each elevation model, mapped canals | made by khg_units_maps in re-peat/tools/project_report_khg.py from the KHG work folder}}

<!-- Writing prompt: How many units each model gives, how their boundaries follow canals and rivers, and where the models draw them differently and why. -->

DeltaDTM gives 30 *sub-KHG* units and DEMNAS 28, with median areas of 31 and 27 km² and ranges of 12 to 100 and 12 to 144 km². Boundaries follow the mapped primary and secondary canals, which are sparse in the centre and north-east, so the large units there are set by terrain divides. Both models draw many small units in the canal-dense south and in the north-west, and DEMNAS produces the largest unit (144 km²), where its raised surface shifts the divides.

DeltaDTM and DEMNAS share 66 % of the area in best-matching units, with an adjusted Rand index of 0.46. The mean climatic balance is negative in February (-44 mm/month) and July (-23 mm/month) and positive in every other month, with the largest surplus in November. February and July are the months with most deficit years in 2018-2025 at Pulau Padang (chapter 8). The KHG mean with MOD16 is 39.1 mm/month on both models (48.1 mm/month with WaPOR) and deficit months per year are 3.17 and 3.15, so the elevation model leaves the balance of the KHG unchanged and moves only the spread between units.

### Priority of sub-KHG

{{figure: khg_priority_maps:14.10.01 | Pulau Padang: need for intervention per sub-KHG, by elevation model. Source: sub-KHG prioritisation with default weights | made by khg_priority_maps in re-peat/tools/project_report_khg.py from the KHG work folder}}

<!-- Writing prompt: Where the high-need units are and what drives them (fire, canals, dryness), and whether the same places come out on top with every model. Mention that the weights are the defaults proposed for agreement with KLH and the World Bank. -->

With the default weights proposed for agreement with KLH and the World Bank, high-need units lie in the north-west, the west and the south on both models, and the north-east and centre are low or medium need. The top-ranked unit differs: DeltaDTM puts it in the south-east and DEMNAS in the north-west, where DeltaDTM ranks a unit third. The need score combines vegetation dryness, fire, peat depth and, where usable, the water balance (chapter 7). Fire is limited on this KHG and the balance order is not usable, so the ranking rests on the remaining components.

{{table: khg_priority_agreement:14.10.01 | Pulau Padang: high-need area and agreement of the priority classes between elevation models. Source: sub-KHG prioritisation with default weights | made by khg_priority_agreement in re-peat/tools/project_report_khg.py from the KHG work folder}}

<!-- Writing prompt: Say how much area keeps its class across models and how strongly the scores agree; explain the largest difference. -->

High-need area is 287 km² (10 units) on DeltaDTM and 291 km² (10 units) on DEMNAS. 71 % of the area keeps its need class between the two models, 72 % of the high-need area in one is also high need in the other, and the area-weighted Spearman correlation of the scores is 0.72. The total hardly moves, but the units differ in shape, so the high-need area is similar while the unit that ranks first is not.

### Canal blocks and pumping

{{figure: khg_blocks_maps:14.10.01 | Pulau Padang: proposed canal blocks in the three highest-ranked sub-KHG, by elevation model. Source: RE-PEAT Interventions on each elevation model, mapped canals and existing blocks | made by khg_blocks_maps in re-peat/tools/project_report_khg.py from the KHG work folder}}

<!-- Writing prompt: Where the blocks go and why there (canal slope, burned land), how many are dropped as low benefit, and how the selected units and block positions change with the model. -->

In the three highest-ranked sub-KHG the *sekat kanal* (canal block) step proposes 49 blocks on DeltaDTM and 42 on DEMNAS, and keeps 24 and 16 for 90 % of the water-level rise; the other 25 and 26 add little and are dropped. Blocks go on canals in the north-west and in the south, where the surface falls along the canal. The selected units differ (S27, S20 and S01 on DeltaDTM; S02, S17 and S18 on DEMNAS), so the two plans cover the same general areas but not the same canals. Existing blocks on the analysed canals number 3 on DeltaDTM and 11 on DEMNAS, out of 156 in the KHG.

<!-- Writing prompt: Give the blocks proposed and kept, water held against the deficit, and the central budget with its main cost item; say what drives the cost differences between models. -->

The kept blocks hold 0.43 million m³ on DeltaDTM and 0.64 on DEMNAS. The deficit of the selected units is 0 million m³ on both, so the water held cannot be set against a deficit. The central budget is 3 billion Rp on DeltaDTM (low 2, high 3) and 11 billion Rp on DEMNAS (low 7, high 11), and neither plan includes revegetation of burned land. The budget is 3.8 times higher on DEMNAS while it keeps fewer blocks (16 against 24), so the cost follows the canals and units selected and hardly the block count. Pumping was not assessed in this analysis.

### How the elevation model changes the result

{{table: khg_dem_effect:14.10.01 | Pulau Padang: headline outputs by elevation model and their spread. Source: RE-PEAT results on each elevation model | made by khg_dem_effect in re-peat/tools/project_report_khg.py from the KHG work folder}}

<!-- Writing prompt: Which outputs move most with the elevation model and which barely move; for this KHG, which model you would use for decisions and why. -->

The balance does not move (39.1 mm/month on both models), and the number of units, the high-need area and the number of high-need units change by a factor of 1.1 or less. Outputs that depend on canal slopes move more: blocks needed by the slope rule of the ranking over the whole KHG are 992 on DeltaDTM and 2,355 on DEMNAS (x2.4), blocks kept and water held x1.5, and the central budget x3.8, partly because the selected units change. There is no LiDAR to check either model. Chapter 4 measured the processed DeltaDTM error at 0.47 to 0.64 m over 1 km at two KHG and processed DEMNAS at 1.66 m over 1 km at Mendahara, so block counts here should be read as a range between the two models, the DeltaDTM count being the nearer to a measured error.

For decisions at Pulau Padang DeltaDTM is the model to use, with canal levels and block crests set by levelling in the field before any design.

### Summary for this KHG

<!-- Writing prompt: Three to five sentences: the main finding for rewetting in this KHG, the recommended elevation model, the proposed blocks and budget, and what is still missing (data or steps not yet run). -->

Pulau Padang is a medium-priority KHG with little fire history. The 3 stations show no dry-season deficit from 1 June to 15 October 2026, although the climatic balance is negative in February and July. On DeltaDTM, the model to use, the plan keeps 24 canal blocks in the three highest-ranked sub-KHG, holding 0.43 million m³ at a central budget of 3 billion Rp, while DEMNAS gives 16 kept blocks and 11 billion Rp. The choice of model changes the selected sub-KHG, so blocks, water and budget move with the units as well as the terrain, while the KHG balance stays the same. Missing are a LiDAR reference for the two models, enough stations to test the order of units, and any pumping assessment.
