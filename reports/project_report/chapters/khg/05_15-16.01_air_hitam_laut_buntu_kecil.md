<!-- Writing prompt: Where the KHG lies, how much of it is peat, how it ranks nationally and why, what canal network and blocks it has, and which elevation models and steps have run. -->

Air Hitam Laut-Buntu Kecil is a coastal *KHG* (*Kesatuan Hidrologis Gambut*, peat hydrological unit) on the border of Jambi and Sumatera Selatan. Of its 1,837 km², 1,432 km² is peat, about 78 % of the area. It ranks 90th of 476 KHG at the default weights proposed for agreement with KLH and the World Bank, in the high class, and stays in that class in 98 % of the random weight draws (chapter 6). Fire history decides the top of the national ranking (chapter 6), and this KHG sits in the lower half of the high class: it is one of four main KHG that leave the top 158 under at least one of the dashboard's weight presets.

The KHG has 873 km of mapped canals (57 km primary, 344 km secondary, 472 km tertiary), 238 existing water-management structures and 201 canal blocks recorded as built. It has 9 SiTMAT water-table stations and a 25 m LiDAR terrain model that covers the whole KHG, and modelled peat depth covers 96 % of it (chapter 3). The full chain has run on all three elevation models: LiDAR, corrected DeltaDTM and corrected DEMNAS.

### Terrain and the three elevation models

{{figure: khg_dem_maps:15-16.01 | Air Hitam Laut-Buntu Kecil: ground elevation from each elevation model on one colour scale, and their differences. Source: LiDAR terrain model, DeltaDTM and DEMNAS after the DEM correction | made by khg_dem_maps in re-peat/tools/project_report_khg.py from the KHG work folder}}

<!-- Writing prompt: Describe the terrain (dome, river levees, coast) and where the models disagree, for example along rivers, plantations or forest edges, and the likely reason (canopy left in the surface, coarse resolution, datum). -->

On LiDAR the peat rises from the north-eastern coast to a broad dome in the west and south-west (elevation 2.0 to 13.0 m between the 5th and 95th percentiles), with lower ground along the estuary that cuts into the KHG from the east and a small high tip in the far south. DeltaDTM draws the same dome and sits a little higher almost everywhere, with a few local patches of larger difference. DEMNAS is higher than LiDAR over most of the dome and the north-east, shows rectangular patches in the west, and falls below LiDAR along the northern and eastern coastal edge. The difference histogram of DEMNAS peaks at +2.75 m and DEMNAS is more than 2 m above LiDAR over 69 % of the KHG, which fits a surface model that keeps part of the canopy over forest and is pieced together from several radar sources (chapter 4).

<!-- Writing prompt: Compare relief and the median and spread of the difference to LiDAR; say whether the difference is a constant offset (datum) or local error, and what that means for the slopes the later steps use. -->

The 5th to 95th percentile range of height is 2.0 to 13.0 m on LiDAR, 2.8 to 13.7 m on DeltaDTM and 1.4 to 16.6 m on DEMNAS, so DEMNAS stretches the relief by about 4 m. DeltaDTM sits a median 0.95 m above LiDAR with a spread (NMAD) of 0.71 m: most of its difference is a near-constant offset, which leaves slopes and divides largely intact. DEMNAS sits 2.96 m above LiDAR with an NMAD of 1.86 m, so much of its difference is local error, and that error goes straight into the canal slopes and head drops the later steps use.

### Sub-KHG units

{{figure: khg_units_maps:15-16.01 | Air Hitam Laut-Buntu Kecil: sub-KHG units delineated on each elevation model. Source: sub-KHG delineation on each elevation model, mapped canals | made by khg_units_maps in re-peat/tools/project_report_khg.py from the KHG work folder}}

<!-- Writing prompt: How many units each model gives, how their boundaries follow canals and rivers, and where the models draw them differently and why. -->

LiDAR and DeltaDTM each give 29 *sub-KHG* units and DEMNAS 30, all with a median area of 56 to 57 km². Where canals are dense, in the west and the north-east, unit boundaries follow the canal grid in steps; in the canal-free centre they follow the terrain and come out as rounder cells. Mapped rivers are missing for this KHG (chapter 3), so no river line fixes a boundary and the units rest on the elevation model and the canals alone. The LiDAR set has the widest size range, 33 to 164 km², including a unit in the south-west with a ragged outline wrapped around a neighbour, likely where the flat dome top lets small height differences decide the flow paths.

<!-- Writing prompt: Say how far the unit sets agree (best-matching area, adjusted Rand index) and which pair differs most. -->

LiDAR shares 67 % of the area with its best-matching unit in either open model, with an adjusted Rand index of 0.54 for both pairs. The two open models agree more with each other (72 %, 0.61) than either does with LiDAR. The units therefore differ most between LiDAR and the open models, and DeltaDTM's smaller error relative to LiDAR does not translate into closer units here.

### Water balance and deficit

{{table: khg_water:15-16.01 | Air Hitam Laut-Buntu Kecil: dry-season deficit from water-table stations and climatic balance per sub-KHG. Source: SiTMAT water-table stations, CHIRPS rain, MOD16 and WaPOR evapotranspiration | made by khg_water in re-peat/tools/project_report_khg.py from the KHG work folder}}

<!-- Writing prompt: State the deficit and its period, the months in deficit, and whether the order of units by balance is usable for each model; explain any model whose order test fails. -->

The *neraca air* (water balance) from 8 water-table stations gives a supply deficit of 211 mm from 1 June to 15 October 2026 (209 to 212 mm for a specific yield of 0.10 to 0.20), or 386 million m³ over the KHG, with 4 months in deficit in that window. This deficit does not depend on the elevation model. The climatic balance of 2018-2025 averages 66.8 to 66.9 mm/month with MOD16 and 81.4 mm/month with WaPOR, with 2.35 to 2.36 deficit months a year. The order of units by balance is not usable on any of the three models, because MOD16 and WaPOR do not order the units' evapotranspiration alike (chapter 8); the balance values are therefore left out of the sub-KHG ranking.

{{figure: khg_water_fig:15-16.01 | Air Hitam Laut-Buntu Kecil: climatic balance through the mean year, and the balance of each sub-KHG, by elevation model. Source: CHIRPS rain and MOD16 evapotranspiration, 2018-2025 | made by khg_water_fig in re-peat/tools/project_report_khg.py from the KHG work folder}}

<!-- Writing prompt: Name the dry months, and say whether the elevation model changes the balance (it enters only through the units). -->

In the mean year July is the only month with a negative balance, and February and August to October stay low but positive; the 2026 dry season was drier than this mean, with 4 months in deficit. The KHG mean is the same on all three models to within 1 mm/month. The models change only the spread between units, 58.8 to 76.6 mm/month on LiDAR against 59.1 to 82.0 mm/month on DeltaDTM and 59.1 to 81.3 mm/month on DEMNAS, where single units reach higher values.

### Priority of sub-KHG

{{figure: khg_priority_maps:15-16.01 | Air Hitam Laut-Buntu Kecil: need for intervention per sub-KHG, by elevation model. Source: sub-KHG prioritisation with default weights | made by khg_priority_maps in re-peat/tools/project_report_khg.py from the KHG work folder}}

<!-- Writing prompt: Where the high-need units are and what drives them (fire, canals, dryness), and whether the same places come out on top with every model. Mention that the weights are the defaults proposed for agreement with KLH and the World Bank. -->

With the default weights proposed for agreement with KLH and the World Bank, the high-need units lie in the west and south-west, where the canal grid is densest, and at the north-eastern tip, which also has canals; the central north, where few canals are mapped, is low need on every model. The same broad pattern appears on all three models. On LiDAR and DeltaDTM two of the three top-ranked units lie in the south-west and the third in the centre south, while DEMNAS places all three in the west, one of them further north.

Every model gives 10 high-need units, covering 777 km² on LiDAR against 645 km² on DeltaDTM and 653 km² on DEMNAS. Between models 73 to 78 % of the area keeps its need class and the area-weighted Spearman correlation of scores is 0.84 to 0.85. The largest difference is the extra 124 to 132 km² of high need on LiDAR, with the same number of units: the class goes to whole units, and LiDAR's high-need units are larger on average (77.7 km² against 64.5 and 65.3 km²).

### Canal blocks and pumping

{{figure: khg_blocks_maps:15-16.01 | Air Hitam Laut-Buntu Kecil: proposed canal blocks in the three highest-ranked sub-KHG, by elevation model. Source: RE-PEAT Interventions on each elevation model, mapped canals and existing blocks | made by khg_blocks_maps in re-peat/tools/project_report_khg.py from the KHG work folder}}

<!-- Writing prompt: Where the blocks go and why there (canal slope, burned land), how many are dropped as low benefit, and how the selected units and block positions change with the model. -->

New *sekat kanal* (canal blocks) can only go on analysed canals, and in the selected units these lie in the south-west, so the unit in the centre south that LiDAR and DeltaDTM select receives none. On LiDAR the proposed blocks cluster on the canal grid in the southern part of the western unit, beside existing blocks, and 19 of 40 are kept. DeltaDTM places blocks on the same grid and on canals further north-west, keeping 18 of 26. DEMNAS selects different units and puts most of its 41 proposed blocks on a canal grid in the northern part of those units, of which only 5 are kept; the rest are dropped as low benefit.

{{table: khg_interventions:15-16.01 | Air Hitam Laut-Buntu Kecil: canal blocks, water held and budget, by elevation model. Source: RE-PEAT Interventions and sub-KHG prioritisation on each elevation model | made by khg_interventions in re-peat/tools/project_report_khg.py from the KHG work folder}}

<!-- Writing prompt: Give the blocks proposed and kept, water held against the deficit, and the central budget with its main cost item; say what drives the cost differences between models. -->

The kept blocks hold 0.74 million m³ on LiDAR, 0.30 million m³ on DeltaDTM and 1.39 million m³ on DEMNAS, against a deficit of the selected units of 38.9, 46.7 and 33.9 million m³: about 1.9 %, 0.6 % and 4.1 % of it. The 5 DEMNAS blocks hold the most water, which matches chapter 9's finding that much of the rise DEMNAS shows along a canal is likely noise. The central budget is 11 billion Rp on LiDAR, 18 billion Rp on DeltaDTM and 26 billion Rp on DEMNAS. Revegetation of burned land makes up 11, 18 and 22 billion Rp of these, so the budget follows how much burned land the selected units hold rather than the number of blocks. Pumping was not assessed in this analysis.

### How the elevation model changes the result

{{table: khg_dem_effect:15-16.01 | Air Hitam Laut-Buntu Kecil: headline outputs by elevation model and their spread. Source: RE-PEAT results on each elevation model | made by khg_dem_effect in re-peat/tools/project_report_khg.py from the KHG work folder}}

<!-- Writing prompt: Which outputs move most with the elevation model and which barely move; for this KHG, which model you would use for decisions and why. -->

The number and size of units, the area they cover, the climatic balance and the number of high-need units barely move with the elevation model, and the station deficit does not depend on it. The outputs that rest on canal slopes move most. Blocks needed by the slope rule of the ranking over the whole KHG range from 1,012 on DeltaDTM to 2,616 on DEMNAS (a factor of 2.6), with LiDAR at 1,543; this is the one KHG of the five where DeltaDTM gives fewer than LiDAR (chapter 10), probably because its 30 m grid smooths canal-scale slopes. Blocks kept differ by a factor of 3.8, water held by 4.6 and the central budget by 2.3, and these also move because each model selects different sub-KHG.

LiDAR covers the whole KHG, so decisions here should rest on the LiDAR results. DeltaDTM is the closer of the two open models in height (an offset of 0.95 m with an NMAD of 0.71 m), but its units agree with LiDAR no better than DEMNAS's do, and DEMNAS's local error of 1.86 m is several times the 0.40 m head step used to place blocks.

### Summary for this KHG

<!-- Writing prompt: Three to five sentences: the main finding for rewetting in this KHG, the recommended elevation model, the proposed blocks and budget, and what is still missing (data or steps not yet run). -->

Air Hitam Laut-Buntu Kecil lost 211 mm of water, 386 million m³, in the 2026 dry season, and the high-need units sit on the dense canal grid of the west and south-west and at the north-eastern tip. On the recommended LiDAR model, RE-PEAT Interventions keeps 19 of 40 proposed blocks in the three top-ranked units; they hold 0.74 million m³, about 1.9 % of those units' deficit, so blocking alone will not close the deficit. The central budget is 11 billion Rp, almost all of it for revegetation of burned land. The choice of elevation model changes the blocks, the water held and the budget by factors of 2.3 to 4.6, partly through different selected units, while the deficit, the balance and the broad pattern of need hardly change. Pumping was not assessed, the order of units by climatic balance could not be used, and mapped rivers are missing for this KHG.
