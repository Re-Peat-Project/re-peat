<!-- Writing prompt: Where the KHG lies, how much of it is peat, how it ranks nationally and why, what canal network and blocks it has, and which elevation models and steps have run. -->

Kampar-Gaung is the largest *KHG* (*Kesatuan Hidrologis Gambut*, peat hydrological unit) analysed in this report: 7,108 km² in Riau, of which 6,863 km² is peat. With the default weights proposed for agreement with KLH and the World Bank it ranks 178 of 476 ranked KHG nationally, in the medium priority class. It is one of the most heavily drained and most heavily managed KHG in the set. The mapped network has 260 km of primary, 1,585 km of secondary and 10,994 km of tertiary canals, the national layer records 2,799 water-management structures, 2,754 of them realised canal blocks, and 92 SiTMAT water-table stations lie inside it. LiDAR covers the whole KHG, so the full chain has run on all three elevation models (LiDAR, DeltaDTM and DEMNAS), from the terrain to the canal blocks, and a station-based deficit exists.

### Terrain and the three elevation models

{{figure: khg_dem_maps:14.02-04-05.01 | Kampar-Gaung: ground elevation from each elevation model on one colour scale, and their differences. Source: LiDAR terrain model, DeltaDTM and DEMNAS after the DEM correction | made by khg_dem_maps in re-peat/tools/project_report_khg.py from the KHG work folder}}

<!-- Writing prompt: Describe the terrain (dome, river levees, coast) and where the models disagree, for example along rivers, plantations or forest edges, and the likely reason (canopy left in the surface, coarse resolution, datum). -->

On LiDAR the ground is highest in the west and south-west of the KHG and falls gently towards the low eastern end, with lower, linear corridors running north-west to south-east across the centre. DeltaDTM reproduces this pattern almost everywhere and sits slightly above LiDAR over most of the KHG, with a stronger positive patch in the east. DEMNAS disagrees in large rectangular patches: it is more than 2 m above LiDAR over 36 % of the KHG, over much of the south and west, and more than 2 m below LiDAR over 3 %, in parts of the north-centre. Straight-edged patches of this kind follow land-use blocks or source tiles rather than terrain, and the likely causes are canopy or plantation stands left in the surface model, which the correction removes only in part (chapter 4), and joins between the radar sources from which DEMNAS is built.

<!-- Writing prompt: Compare relief and the median and spread of the difference to LiDAR; say whether the difference is a constant offset (datum) or local error, and what that means for the slopes the later steps use. -->

The 5th to 95th percentile of elevation spans 8.5 m on LiDAR (0.9 to 9.4 m), 9.0 m on DeltaDTM and 11.5 m on DEMNAS (differences of the values in the table). DeltaDTM lies a median 0.91 m above LiDAR with a spread (NMAD) of 0.74 m, so most of its difference is an offset; the LiDAR datum was never recorded, so the offset cannot be assigned to either side (chapter 4), and it does not change slopes. DEMNAS lies a median 1.31 m above LiDAR but with a spread of 1.87 m, larger than the median itself. Its difference is mainly local error, and local error is what changes the canal slopes and head drops that the block design reads from the terrain.

### Sub-KHG units

{{figure: khg_units_maps:14.02-04-05.01 | Kampar-Gaung: sub-KHG units delineated on each elevation model. Source: sub-KHG delineation on each elevation model, mapped canals | made by khg_units_maps in re-peat/tools/project_report_khg.py from the KHG work folder}}

<!-- Writing prompt: How many units each model gives, how their boundaries follow canals and rivers, and where the models draw them differently and why. -->

The delineation gives 28 *sub-KHG* units on LiDAR, 27 on DeltaDTM and 29 on DEMNAS, with median sizes of 215 to 225 km². The units are large because the KHG has 977 primary and secondary canal segments over its whole area (chapter 5), far fewer per km² than the South Sumatra and Jambi KHG. In the canal-dense north and centre the unit boundaries follow the primary and secondary canals and look alike on all three models. Where the canals are sparse, in the south and east, the boundaries follow terrain divides, and that is where the models draw them differently.

LiDAR and DeltaDTM agree best: 80 % of the area lies in the best-matching unit, with an adjusted Rand index of 0.66. LiDAR and DEMNAS differ most, at 72 % and 0.57, and DeltaDTM and DEMNAS fall between, at 76 % and 0.60. The ordering follows the local error of the elevation models in the previous table.

### Water balance and deficit

{{table: khg_water:14.02-04-05.01 | Kampar-Gaung: dry-season deficit from water-table stations and climatic balance per sub-KHG. Source: SiTMAT water-table stations, CHIRPS rain, MOD16 and WaPOR evapotranspiration | made by khg_water in re-peat/tools/project_report_khg.py from the KHG work folder}}

<!-- Writing prompt: State the deficit and its period, the months in deficit, and whether the order of units by balance is usable for each model; explain any model whose order test fails. -->

From 1 June to 9 October 2026, 78 stations give a *neraca air* (water balance) deficit of 7 mm (2 to 12 mm over the range of specific yield), about 50 million m³ over the KHG, with 2 months in deficit. The deficit is small because the climate was close to neutral in that season and the station mean water table rose by 10.9 cm while the stations were held at about 40 cm (chapter 8). The deficit does not depend on the elevation model. The order of sub-KHG by climatic balance is not usable with any of the three unit sets, because MOD16 and WaPOR evapotranspiration rank the units in opposite directions (Spearman -0.48 to -0.59, chapter 8).

{{figure: khg_water_fig:14.02-04-05.01 | Kampar-Gaung: climatic balance through the mean year, and the balance of each sub-KHG, by elevation model. Source: CHIRPS rain and MOD16 evapotranspiration, 2018-2025 | made by khg_water_fig in re-peat/tools/project_report_khg.py from the KHG work folder}}

<!-- Writing prompt: Name the dry months, and say whether the elevation model changes the balance (it enters only through the units). -->

Over 2018-2025 the KHG has two dry months a year on average: July is the driest (a mean of -25 mm) and February has a second, smaller deficit (-13 mm, chapter 8). The KHG mean is 58.6 mm/month on all three models, and the deficit months per year differ only between 2.65 and 2.66. The elevation model enters only through the unit boundaries, so it changes the spread of unit means (50.9 to 64.5 mm/month on LiDAR, 50.9 to 67.2 on DeltaDTM, 50.5 to 63.8 on DEMNAS), and that spread is small next to the disagreement between the two evapotranspiration products.

### Priority of sub-KHG

{{figure: khg_priority_maps:14.02-04-05.01 | Kampar-Gaung: need for intervention per sub-KHG, by elevation model. Source: sub-KHG prioritisation with default weights | made by khg_priority_maps in re-peat/tools/project_report_khg.py from the KHG work folder}}

<!-- Writing prompt: Where the high-need units are and what drives them (fire, canals, dryness), and whether the same places come out on top with every model. Mention that the weights are the defaults proposed for agreement with KLH and the World Bank. -->

With the default weights proposed for agreement with KLH and the World Bank, the high-need units lie in the west, the south-west lobe and the south-centre of the KHG, and the eastern end is mostly low need. The three top-ranked units are in the same three places on every model: a long strip in the north-centre, the south-west corner and the north-east. All three lie outside concession areas, and each has burned more than most units, has few existing blocks for the blocks its canals need, or has deep peat. DEMNAS draws the north-central and north-eastern top units larger and adds more high-need area in the north.

{{table: khg_priority_agreement:14.02-04-05.01 | Kampar-Gaung: high-need area and agreement of the priority classes between elevation models. Source: sub-KHG prioritisation with default weights | made by khg_priority_agreement in re-peat/tools/project_report_khg.py from the KHG work folder}}

<!-- Writing prompt: Say how much area keeps its class across models and how strongly the scores agree; explain the largest difference. -->

LiDAR and DeltaDTM give almost the same high-need area (2,398 and 2,384 km², 10 and 9 units), and 77 % of the area keeps its class between them (area-weighted Spearman 0.81). DEMNAS gives 3,003 km² of high need. The largest difference is between LiDAR and DEMNAS: only 63 % of the area keeps its class (Spearman 0.75), although 74 % of the LiDAR high-need area is also high need on DEMNAS. Most of the disagreement is therefore in the medium and low classes. The extra DEMNAS high-need area follows its larger units, and its noisier terrain raises the number of blocks the canals appear to need, which feeds the missing-block indicator.

### Canal blocks and pumping

{{figure: khg_blocks_maps:14.02-04-05.01 | Kampar-Gaung: proposed canal blocks in the three highest-ranked sub-KHG, by elevation model. Source: RE-PEAT Interventions on each elevation model, mapped canals and existing blocks | made by khg_blocks_maps in re-peat/tools/project_report_khg.py from the KHG work folder}}

<!-- Writing prompt: Where the blocks go and why there (canal slope, burned land), how many are dropped as low benefit, and how the selected units and block positions change with the model. -->

The selected sub-KHG are in the same three places on every model, but their outlines differ, and DEMNAS draws the north-central and north-eastern units larger. On LiDAR the head rule proposes 51 *sekat kanal* (canal blocks) and keeps 24. DeltaDTM proposes 117 and keeps 57, and DEMNAS proposes 194 and keeps 33. The kept blocks sit on the canals of the south-west corner and the north-east, where the land rises along the canal. The north-central strip already holds a line of existing blocks, and several new blocks proposed there are dropped as low benefit. DEMNAS spreads proposals over many more canals, and its balance step keeps only 17 % of them (33 of 194, a ratio of the table values), because much of the rise it reads along a canal is likely terrain noise (chapter 9). Pumping was not assessed in this analysis.

{{table: khg_interventions:14.02-04-05.01 | Kampar-Gaung: canal blocks, water held and budget, by elevation model. Source: RE-PEAT Interventions and sub-KHG prioritisation on each elevation model | made by khg_interventions in re-peat/tools/project_report_khg.py from the KHG work folder}}

<!-- Writing prompt: Give the blocks proposed and kept, water held against the deficit, and the central budget with its main cost item; say what drives the cost differences between models. -->

The kept blocks hold 2.33 million m³ on LiDAR, 4.71 on DeltaDTM and 10.02 on DEMNAS, against a 2026 deficit of the selected units of 5.1, 5.0 and 7.5 million m³. That is about 46 %, 94 % and 134 % of the deficit (ratios of the table values). The DEMNAS figure exceeds the deficit, which shows how much head its noise adds. The central budget is 1 billion rupiah on LiDAR, 7 on DeltaDTM and 38 on DEMNAS. On every model the largest item is the estimate, from the KLH adequacy index (IPG), of blocks missing in the sub-KHG outside the three analysed in detail, and no revegetation of burned land enters. That estimate grows with the number of blocks the slope rule of the ranking requires, which is 1,675 for the whole KHG on LiDAR, 3,394 on DeltaDTM and 9,518 on DEMNAS. On LiDAR the requirement is below the 2,754 blocks already built, which is why so little new spending is proposed there.

### How the elevation model changes the result

{{table: khg_dem_effect:14.02-04-05.01 | Kampar-Gaung: headline outputs by elevation model and their spread. Source: RE-PEAT results on each elevation model | made by khg_dem_effect in re-peat/tools/project_report_khg.py from the KHG work folder}}

<!-- Writing prompt: Which outputs move most with the elevation model and which barely move; for this KHG, which model you would use for decisions and why. -->

The number and size of units, the area covered and the climatic balance barely move with the elevation model (spread ×1.1 or less, balance range 0.0 mm/month), and the high-need area moves by ×1.3. Everything that reads canal slopes moves a great deal. The blocks required by the slope rule of the ranking spread ×5.7, proposed blocks ×3.8, kept blocks ×2.4, water held ×4.3 and the central budget ×27.2. Part of the block, water and budget spread comes from DEMNAS selecting larger units (their deficit is 7.5 against 5.0 to 5.1 million m³), and the rest comes from the terrain itself: DEMNAS's local error of 1.87 m NMAD is several times the 0.40 m head step a block is designed to hold. Kampar-Gaung is fully covered by LiDAR, so LiDAR should be used for decisions here. DeltaDTM is the closest substitute (an offset of 0.91 m, spread 0.74 m, unit agreement 80 %), but it still doubles the kept blocks and water held and raises the central budget from 1 to 7 billion rupiah. DEMNAS should not be used for block design at this KHG.

### Summary for this KHG

<!-- Writing prompt: Three to five sentences: the main finding for rewetting in this KHG, the recommended elevation model, the proposed blocks and budget, and what is still missing (data or steps not yet run). -->

Kampar-Gaung is densely canalised and already has 2,754 realised blocks, which on LiDAR is more than the 1,675 the slope rule of the ranking requires. In 2026 its stations were held near 40 cm with a dry-season deficit of only 7 mm, so the case for new blocks rests on the highest-ranked units, which lie outside concessions in the south-west, the north-centre and the north-east. On LiDAR, the recommended model here, the plan for the three highest-ranked sub-KHG keeps 24 new blocks that hold 2.33 million m³, about half the deficit of those units, for a central budget of about 1 billion rupiah. DeltaDTM and especially DEMNAS inflate blocks, water held and cost (the central budget on DEMNAS is 27.2 times that on LiDAR), while the units, the balance and the location of the top-priority units hold steady. Pumping was not assessed in this analysis, the order of units by climatic balance cannot be used because the evapotranspiration products disagree, and the station deficit covers a single dry season (chapter 8).
