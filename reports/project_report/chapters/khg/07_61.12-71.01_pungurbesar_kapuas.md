<!-- Writing prompt: Where the KHG lies, how much of it is peat, how it ranks nationally and why, what canal network and blocks it has, and which elevation models and steps have run. -->

Pungurbesar-Kapuas is a *KHG* (*Kesatuan Hidrologis Gambut*, peat hydrological unit) in Kalimantan Barat. It covers 1,013 km², of which 744 km² is peat. It ranks 1 of 476 ranked KHG nationally (high priority) with the default weights proposed for agreement with KLH and the World Bank, and stays in the top 7 when only fire history is weighed (chapter 6). Peat here burns in most years: 16.6 % of it in 2018, 11.0 % in 2023 and 8.0 % in the part of 2026 covered so far, and 1,825 hotspots fell on peat this year, 710 of them in March (chapter 6). The mapped network is dense, with 379 km of primary, 1,537 km of secondary and 2,871 km of tertiary canals, and the national layer records 521 water-management structures, 395 of them realised canal blocks. No LiDAR covers this KHG, so the chain ran on DeltaDTM and DEMNAS only, both after the DEM correction. Five SiTMAT water-table stations lie inside the KHG, and three of them give the station deficit.

### Terrain and the three elevation models

{{figure: khg_dem_maps:61.12-71.01 | Pungurbesar-Kapuas: ground elevation from each elevation model on one colour scale, and their differences. Source: LiDAR terrain model, DeltaDTM and DEMNAS after the DEM correction | made by khg_dem_maps in re-peat/tools/project_report_khg.py from the KHG work folder}}

<!-- Writing prompt: Describe the terrain (dome, river levees, coast) and where the models disagree, for example along rivers, plantations or forest edges, and the likely reason (canopy left in the surface, coarse resolution, datum). -->

On DeltaDTM the KHG is a peat plain with a raised area in the centre and a second one towards the south-east along the long axis of the KHG, and the lowest ground lies in the north-west and at the south-eastern tip. DEMNAS shows the same pattern with more contrast: its elevation runs from -0.9 to 11.5 m between the 5th and 95th percentiles, against 1.2 to 8.4 m on DeltaDTM. The difference map shows blocky patches where DEMNAS stands above DeltaDTM, and the opposite sign along the western and south-western margin and in the north. DEMNAS is more than 2 m above DeltaDTM over 22 % of the KHG and more than 2 m below it over 18 %. Patches of this shape follow canopy and land cover more than terrain; at the two LiDAR KHG the correction removed only part of the canopy from DEMNAS, which stayed worst under tree cover and cropland (chapter 4). The same cause is the likely one here, but with no LiDAR it cannot be tested.

<!-- Writing prompt: Compare relief and the median and spread of the difference to LiDAR; say whether the difference is a constant offset (datum) or local error, and what that means for the slopes the later steps use. -->

Relief between the 5th and 95th percentiles is 1.2 to 8.4 m on DeltaDTM (7.2 m) and -0.9 to 11.5 m on DEMNAS (12.4 m, 1.7 times wider). With no LiDAR there is no difference to LiDAR to report, so the error of each model cannot be measured here. At the two KHG that do have LiDAR, the processed models sat 0.6 to 1.0 m above the LiDAR as a constant offset that does not change slopes, and the error of DeltaDTM over 1 km was 0.64 and 0.47 m, against 1.66 and 2.19 m for DEMNAS (chapter 4). If the same holds here, DEMNAS slopes are the less reliable input to the delineation and the canal-block step, and block counts from the two models should be read as a range.

### Sub-KHG units

{{figure: khg_units_maps:61.12-71.01 | Pungurbesar-Kapuas: sub-KHG units delineated on each elevation model. Source: sub-KHG delineation on each elevation model, mapped canals | made by khg_units_maps in re-peat/tools/project_report_khg.py from the KHG work folder}}

<!-- Writing prompt: How many units each model gives, how their boundaries follow canals and rivers, and where the models draw them differently and why. -->

DeltaDTM gives 30 *sub-KHG* units and DEMNAS 29, with median areas of 28 and 31 km² and ranges of 18 to 93 and 19 to 92 km². The units are far smaller than at Sugihan-Lumpur because the canal network is dense (4,787 km of canals in 1,013 km², about 4.7 km per km², a ratio of the figures shown), and most unit boundaries follow the canal lines that run across the KHG. The two models draw the larger units in the north-west and around the central dome differently, where canals are further apart and terrain divides decide the boundary.

<!-- Writing prompt: Say how far the unit sets agree (best-matching area, adjusted Rand index) and which pair differs most. -->

DeltaDTM and DEMNAS share 88 % of the area in best-matching units, with an adjusted Rand index of 0.79. This is higher than the 0.60 to 0.66 found between the three models at Sugihan-Lumpur (chapter 5), which fits the denser canal network here: the canals fix most of the layout and the terrain moves only some divides. There is no LiDAR layout to compare with, so the agreement shows that the choice of model matters little for the units, and does not show that either is correct.

### Water balance and deficit

From 1 June to 15 October 2026, three stations give a dry-season deficit of 132 mm (122 to 143 mm for a specific yield of 0.10 to 0.20), or 134 million m³ over the KHG, with 3 months in deficit in that window. The deficit does not depend on the elevation model. With only three stations it describes the managed land around them and cannot show differences between sub-KHG. The order of units by climatic balance is usable on both unit sets, because the two evapotranspiration products agree, so no model fails the test here.

<!-- Writing prompt: Name the dry months, and say whether the elevation model changes the balance (it enters only through the units). -->

In the mean year the KHG balance is lowest in February (13 mm/month) and July (36 mm/month), and stays above zero in every month. In single years it is negative in 1.42 months (DeltaDTM units) to 1.45 months (DEMNAS units) on average. The KHG mean is 91.3 mm/month with MOD16 and 123.3 mm/month with WaPOR on both unit sets, so the elevation model does not change the balance of the KHG. It changes the spread between units slightly, from 78.3 to 132.0 mm/month on DeltaDTM units to 76.2 to 129.0 mm/month on DEMNAS units.

<!-- Writing prompt: Where the high-need units are and what drives them (fire, canals, dryness), and whether the same places come out on top with every model. Mention that the weights are the defaults proposed for agreement with KLH and the World Bank. -->

With the default weights proposed for agreement with KLH and the World Bank, high-need units form a band through the centre and the south-east on both models, and the north-west is mostly low need. The three top-ranked units lie next to each other in the centre, at the same places on both models. This pattern is consistent with the fire history of the KHG (chapter 6) and with the need score of chapter 7, which combines vegetation dryness, fire, peat depth and, where it is usable, the water balance.

High-need area is 379 km² (10 units) on DeltaDTM and 363 km² (10 units) on DEMNAS. Of the area both models cover, 84 % keeps its need class, 80 % of the high-need area in one model is also high need in the other, and the area-weighted Spearman correlation of the scores is 0.94. The few units that change class lie at the edges of the high-need band and in the north-west, where the two models draw the unit boundaries differently.

### Canal blocks and pumping

{{figure: khg_blocks_maps:61.12-71.01 | Pungurbesar-Kapuas: proposed canal blocks in the three highest-ranked sub-KHG, by elevation model. Source: RE-PEAT Interventions on each elevation model, mapped canals and existing blocks | made by khg_blocks_maps in re-peat/tools/project_report_khg.py from the KHG work folder}}

<!-- Writing prompt: Where the blocks go and why there (canal slope, burned land), how many are dropped as low benefit, and how the selected units and block positions change with the model. -->

The three selected sub-KHG lie in the centre-north of the KHG: S12, S13 and S14 on DeltaDTM, and S12, S14 and S17 on DEMNAS, so two of the three are shared. The *sekat kanal* (canal block) step proposes 143 blocks on DeltaDTM and 140 on DEMNAS along the primary and secondary canals where the canal falls, and keeps 36 and 17 of them for 90 % of the water-level rise; the other 107 and 123 add little and are dropped. The proposed blocks sit mostly along the outer canals of the units and on a few cross canals, and 15 existing blocks lie on the analysed canals (of 395 realised blocks in the KHG). The head-rule count is almost the same on both models, but DEMNAS keeps half as many blocks, so more of its proposed blocks add little rise.

{{table: khg_interventions:61.12-71.01 | Pungurbesar-Kapuas: canal blocks, water held and budget, by elevation model. Source: RE-PEAT Interventions and sub-KHG prioritisation on each elevation model | made by khg_interventions in re-peat/tools/project_report_khg.py from the KHG work folder}}

<!-- Writing prompt: Give the blocks proposed and kept, water held against the deficit, and the central budget with its main cost item; say what drives the cost differences between models. -->

The kept blocks hold 4.25 million m³ on DeltaDTM and 7.41 million m³ on DEMNAS, against a deficit of the selected units of 12.5 and 12.7 million m³, or 34 % and 58 % of it (ratios of the figures shown). The central budget is 34 billion Rp on DeltaDTM and 193 billion Rp on DEMNAS (low estimates 23 and 129 billion Rp). In both plans the main item is the KLH estimate of missing blocks in the sub-KHG outside the analysed units: 1,866 blocks for 33.6 billion Rp on DeltaDTM and 10,687 blocks for 192.4 billion Rp on DEMNAS. The kept blocks cost 0.65 and 0.31 billion Rp, and no revegetation of burned land is priced on either model. The budget therefore follows the slope-based block count of the ranking over the whole KHG, 4,896 on DeltaDTM against 17,432 on DEMNAS, more than the blocks the plan keeps. Pumping was not assessed in this analysis.

### How the elevation model changes the result

{{table: khg_dem_effect:61.12-71.01 | Pungurbesar-Kapuas: headline outputs by elevation model and their spread. Source: RE-PEAT results on each elevation model | made by khg_dem_effect in re-peat/tools/project_report_khg.py from the KHG work folder}}

<!-- Writing prompt: Which outputs move most with the elevation model and which barely move; for this KHG, which model you would use for decisions and why. -->

The balance does not move (91.3 mm/month on both), and the number of units, their median area, the high-need area and the number of high-need units change by a factor of 1.1 or less. The outputs that depend on canal slope move most: blocks needed by the slope rule over the whole KHG differ by ×3.6 (4,896 against 17,432), blocks kept by ×2.1 and the central budget by ×5.6 (34 against 193 billion Rp). Blocks proposed by the head rule barely move (143 and 140), and water held differs by ×1.7. Blocks, water and budget refer to the three top-ranked units, which differ by one unit between the models, so they move with the choice of units as well as with the terrain.

For decisions at Pungurbesar-Kapuas the DeltaDTM plan is the safer base. At the two KHG with LiDAR its error over 1 km was 0.47 to 0.64 m against 1.66 to 2.19 m for DEMNAS (chapter 4), and chapter 9 concludes that DEMNAS block counts should not be taken as a design. This KHG has no LiDAR, so that error is not measured here, and the block counts should be read as a range between the two plans: 17 to 36 blocks kept and 4.25 to 7.41 million m³ held in the selected units.

### Summary for this KHG

<!-- Writing prompt: Three to five sentences: the main finding for rewetting in this KHG, the recommended elevation model, the proposed blocks and budget, and what is still missing (data or steps not yet run). -->

Pungurbesar-Kapuas ranks first nationally and is a densely drained peat KHG that burns in most years; in the 2026 dry season the managed land around three stations needed about 132 mm, or 134 million m³ over the KHG, of supplied water to hold its level. On DeltaDTM, the model to prefer, the plan keeps 36 canal blocks in the three highest-ranked sub-KHG; they hold 4.25 million m³, about a third of the deficit of those units, and the central budget is 34 billion Rp, mostly the estimated cost of blocks in the other sub-KHG. The choice of elevation model changes the units selected and with them the blocks (17 to 36), water held (4.25 to 7.41 million m³) and budget (34 to 193 billion Rp), while the unit layout (88 % agreement), the need class (84 % agreement) and the water balance barely change. Still missing are a LiDAR reference to measure the error of both models, more than three stations for the deficit, and an assessment of pumping.
