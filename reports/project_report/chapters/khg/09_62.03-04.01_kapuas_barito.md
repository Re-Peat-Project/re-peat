<!-- Writing prompt: Where the KHG lies, how much of it is peat, how it ranks nationally and why, what canal network and blocks it has, and which elevation models and steps have run. -->

Kapuas-Barito is a *KHG* (*Kesatuan Hidrologis Gambut*, peat hydrological unit) in Kalimantan Tengah. It covers 5,574 km², of which 4,407 km² is peat. It ranks 96 of 476 ranked KHG nationally (high priority) with the default weights proposed for agreement with KLH and the World Bank, and falls in the high class in 0.99 of the weight draws (chapter 6). The mapped network has 1,501 km of primary, 184 km of secondary and 4,133 km of tertiary canals, and the national layer records 254 water-management structures, 53 of them realised canal blocks. No LiDAR covers this KHG, and 6 SiTMAT water-table stations lie inside it. The chain therefore ran on corrected DeltaDTM and corrected DEMNAS only, with no reference against which to measure the error of either, and a station deficit could be computed. There is no modelled peat-depth layer for this KHG (chapter 3), so the ranking gives peat depth no weight.

### Terrain and the three elevation models

{{figure: khg_dem_maps:62.03-04.01 | Kapuas-Barito: ground elevation from each elevation model on one colour scale, and their differences. Source: LiDAR terrain model, DeltaDTM and DEMNAS after the DEM correction | made by khg_dem_maps in re-peat/tools/project_report_khg.py from the KHG work folder}}

<!-- Writing prompt: Describe the terrain (dome, river levees, coast) and where the models disagree, for example along rivers, plantations or forest edges, and the likely reason (canopy left in the surface, coarse resolution, datum). -->

Both models show a peat landscape that rises from the south to a high northern arm. Elevation between the 5th and 95th percentiles is 2.0 to 24.8 m on DeltaDTM and 0.1 to 34.5 m on DEMNAS, highest in the north, so the relief is much larger than at the two LiDAR KHG. DeltaDTM minus DEMNAS has a 5th to 95th percentile of -10.9 to +3.4 m and its histogram peaks at -5.25 m: DEMNAS is more than 2 m above DeltaDTM over 57 % of the KHG, and stands higher than DeltaDTM across the northern high ground, while DeltaDTM is higher in a patch at the south-western tip. DeltaDTM is defined only for land below 10 m above mean sea level (chapter 4), and much of the northern part lies above that, so the difference there says more about the limit of DeltaDTM than about DEMNAS. The blocky patches in the central and southern DEMNAS are consistent with canopy and cropland left in a surface model, but with no LiDAR here this cannot be tested.

<!-- Writing prompt: Compare relief and the median and spread of the difference to LiDAR; say whether the difference is a constant offset (datum) or local error, and what that means for the slopes the later steps use. -->

Relief between the 5th and 95th percentiles is 2.0 to 24.8 m on DeltaDTM and 0.1 to 34.5 m on DEMNAS, both covering the whole KHG (DeltaDTM resolution 30 m, DEMNAS 8 m). No difference to LiDAR can be given, so it is unknown whether the gap between the models is a constant offset or local error. The two LiDAR KHG suggest local error for DEMNAS: after correction its error over 1 km was 1.66 m at Mendahara-Batanghari against 0.64 m for DeltaDTM (chapter 4). Even the 1 km error of DeltaDTM there (0.47 to 0.64 m across the two benchmark KHG) is above the real 1 km LiDAR relief of 0.38 to 0.46 m, so slopes from either model should be read as a range, not as measured values.

### Sub-KHG units

{{figure: khg_units_maps:62.03-04.01 | Kapuas-Barito: sub-KHG units delineated on each elevation model. Source: sub-KHG delineation on each elevation model, mapped canals | made by khg_units_maps in re-peat/tools/project_report_khg.py from the KHG work folder}}

<!-- Writing prompt: How many units each model gives, how their boundaries follow canals and rivers, and where the models draw them differently and why. -->

DeltaDTM gives 28 *sub-KHG* units and DEMNAS 29. The boundaries follow the dense grid of canals in the west-centre and the canal fans at the southern tip, and a long east-west boundary line appears on both models. In the north, where the maps show no mapped primary or secondary canals, the boundaries follow terrain divides and are drawn differently by the two models.

DeltaDTM and DEMNAS share 68 % of the area in best-matching units, with an adjusted Rand index of 0.46. This is lower than the 0.60 to 0.66 at Sugihan-Lumpur, where the canal network sets most of the layout (chapter 5). Here much of the KHG has few mapped canals and strong relief, so the terrain model has more influence on where units are drawn. Median unit area is 163 km² on DeltaDTM and 156 km² on DEMNAS, with ranges of 102 to 653 km² and 77 to 702 km².

### Water balance and deficit

{{table: khg_water:62.03-04.01 | Kapuas-Barito: dry-season deficit from water-table stations and climatic balance per sub-KHG. Source: SiTMAT water-table stations, CHIRPS rain, MOD16 and WaPOR evapotranspiration | made by khg_water in re-peat/tools/project_report_khg.py from the KHG work folder}}

<!-- Writing prompt: State the deficit and its period, the months in deficit, and whether the order of units by balance is usable for each model; explain any model whose order test fails. -->

From 1 June to 15 October 2026, 6 stations give a dry-season deficit of 299 mm (297 to 301 mm for a specific yield of 0.10 to 0.20), or 1,666 million m³ over the KHG, with 4 months in deficit in that window. The deficit does not depend on the elevation model. The order of units by climatic balance is not usable on either model. The two evapotranspiration products agree at 0.33 against a threshold of 0.45 on DeltaDTM units, and at 0.42 against 0.43 on DEMNAS units, a near miss. The mean balance of the units follows forest share (correlation -0.78 on both models) while rain minus evapotranspiration barely does (0.11 and 0.25), so the order reflects land cover more than water. The balance values are therefore left out of the sub-KHG ranking.

{{figure: khg_water_fig:62.03-04.01 | Kapuas-Barito: climatic balance through the mean year, and the balance of each sub-KHG, by elevation model. Source: CHIRPS rain and MOD16 evapotranspiration, 2018-2025 | made by khg_water_fig in re-peat/tools/project_report_khg.py from the KHG work folder}}

<!-- Writing prompt: Name the dry months, and say whether the elevation model changes the balance (it enters only through the units). -->

The mean climatic balance is negative in July, August and September and positive in the other months, with a peak of 152 mm/month in March. The KHG mean is 72.2 mm/month (MOD16) and 93.5 mm/month (WaPOR) on both models, with 2.34 and 2.35 deficit months a year, so the elevation model does not change the balance of the KHG. It changes only the spread between units, 64.0 to 81.5 mm/month on DeltaDTM and 65.4 to 80.3 mm/month on DEMNAS.

### Priority of sub-KHG

{{figure: khg_priority_maps:62.03-04.01 | Kapuas-Barito: need for intervention per sub-KHG, by elevation model. Source: sub-KHG prioritisation with default weights | made by khg_priority_maps in re-peat/tools/project_report_khg.py from the KHG work folder}}

<!-- Writing prompt: Where the high-need units are and what drives them (fire, canals, dryness), and whether the same places come out on top with every model. Mention that the weights are the defaults proposed for agreement with KLH and the World Bank. -->

With the default weights proposed for agreement with KLH and the World Bank, high-need units lie in the southern half on both models, in the western canal grid and the south-east, and the north is mostly low need. The score uses 13 indicators, mainly fire history, hotspots, vegetation dryness, canal density by class and the gap in canal blocks; it uses neither peat depth nor the water balance. The three top-ranked units have burned in 12 to 31 % of their area on DeltaDTM and 12 to 48 % on DEMNAS, against 4 to 6 % in the median unit. The rank 1 unit differs: DeltaDTM puts it in the central south, DEMNAS in the east. Both put the western canal grid among the top three.

<!-- Writing prompt: Say how much area keeps its class across models and how strongly the scores agree; explain the largest difference. -->

High-need area is 1,632 km² (10 units) on DeltaDTM and 1,391 km² (10 units) on DEMNAS. 81 % of the area keeps its need class between the two models, 81 % of the high-need area of one is also high need in the other, and the area-weighted Spearman correlation of the scores is 0.93. That is higher than the 57 to 65 % at Sugihan-Lumpur, probably because the north-south gradient in fire and canals sets the pattern on both models. The 241 km² difference in high-need area comes from units whose boundaries move, for example the single medium-need unit that DEMNAS draws in the north-east.

### Canal blocks and pumping

{{figure: khg_blocks_maps:62.03-04.01 | Kapuas-Barito: proposed canal blocks in the three highest-ranked sub-KHG, by elevation model. Source: RE-PEAT Interventions on each elevation model, mapped canals and existing blocks | made by khg_blocks_maps in re-peat/tools/project_report_khg.py from the KHG work folder}}

<!-- Writing prompt: Where the blocks go and why there (canal slope, burned land), how many are dropped as low benefit, and how the selected units and block positions change with the model. -->

In the three highest-ranked sub-KHG the *sekat kanal* (canal block) step proposes 128 blocks on DeltaDTM and 110 on DEMNAS, and keeps 44 and 20 for 90 % of the water-level rise; the other 84 and 90 add little and are dropped. The blocks sit close together on the canals of the western grid and along the straight canals of the eastern units. The selected units differ (S21, S19 and S20 on DeltaDTM; S21, S24 and S19 on DEMNAS, where labels belong to each model), but the western canal grid is chosen on both. Of the 53 existing blocks in the KHG, only 2 lie on the analysed canals. With no LiDAR to check either model, block counts here should be read as a range between the two.

{{table: khg_interventions:62.03-04.01 | Kapuas-Barito: canal blocks, water held and budget, by elevation model. Source: RE-PEAT Interventions and sub-KHG prioritisation on each elevation model | made by khg_interventions in re-peat/tools/project_report_khg.py from the KHG work folder}}

<!-- Writing prompt: Give the blocks proposed and kept, water held against the deficit, and the central budget with its main cost item; say what drives the cost differences between models. -->

The kept blocks hold 6.93 million m³ on DeltaDTM and 7.03 million m³ on DEMNAS, against a deficit of the selected units of 119.7 and 119.9 million m³, or 5.8 % and 5.9 % of it. The central budget is 24 billion Rp on DeltaDTM (low to high 13 to 25) and 86 billion Rp on DEMNAS (54 to 90). On DEMNAS the largest item is the estimate of missing blocks in units outside the three analysed, 60.1 billion Rp for 3,337 blocks against 9.8 billion Rp for 542 blocks on DeltaDTM; it follows the slope rule and so the terrain model. Revegetation of burned land is 3.6 billion Rp (300 ha) on DeltaDTM and 17.1 billion Rp (1,409 ha) on DEMNAS, and the new blocks cost under 0.8 billion Rp on both. Pumping was not assessed in this analysis.

### How the elevation model changes the result

{{table: khg_dem_effect:62.03-04.01 | Kapuas-Barito: headline outputs by elevation model and their spread. Source: RE-PEAT results on each elevation model | made by khg_dem_effect in re-peat/tools/project_report_khg.py from the KHG work folder}}

<!-- Writing prompt: Which outputs move most with the elevation model and which barely move; for this KHG, which model you would use for decisions and why. -->

The KHG balance does not move (72.2 mm/month on both), and the number of units, the high-need area and the number of high-need units change by a factor of 1.2 or less. The outputs that depend on canal slope move most. Blocks needed by the slope rule of the ranking over the whole KHG rise from 3,810 on DeltaDTM to 12,184 on DEMNAS (×3.2). Over the 1,685 km of primary and secondary canals that is one block every 442 m against one every 138 m. The median slope along canals in the top three units is 0.055 to 0.085 % on DeltaDTM and 0.12 to 0.25 % on DEMNAS, which fits a rougher DEMNAS surface. The central budget moves ×3.6 and blocks kept ×2.2, while blocks proposed (×1.2) and water held (×1.0) barely change.

For decisions at Kapuas-Barito DeltaDTM is the better choice, since it came closer to LiDAR at both benchmark KHG (chapter 4) and DEMNAS block counts should not be taken as a design (chapter 9). Its limits here are the 10 m ceiling of its definition over the northern high ground and the lack of any local reference, so the three selected units, which lie in the lower southern half, are the part to trust. Block counts and costs should be quoted as a range between the two models.

### Summary for this KHG

<!-- Writing prompt: Three to five sentences: the main finding for rewetting in this KHG, the recommended elevation model, the proposed blocks and budget, and what is still missing (data or steps not yet run). -->

Kapuas-Barito has a station deficit of 299 mm, or 1,666 million m³, in the 2026 dry season, and the order of its sub-KHG by climatic balance is not usable on either model. The ranking points to the western canal grid and the south-east, and the two models agree on the need class of 81 % of the area. On DeltaDTM, the model to use here, the plan keeps 44 canal blocks in the three highest-ranked sub-KHG; they hold 6.93 million m³, about 6 % of the deficit of those units, at a central budget of 24 billion Rp, against 20 blocks and 86 billion Rp on DEMNAS. Most of the spread between models comes from canal slope, which sets the blocks needed (3,810 against 12,184) and the cost of missing blocks. Still missing are LiDAR to check the models, any peat-depth layer, a usable water-balance order and an assessment of pumping.
