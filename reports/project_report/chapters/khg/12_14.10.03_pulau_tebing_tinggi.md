<!-- Writing prompt: Where the KHG lies, how much of it is peat, how it ranks nationally and why, what canal network and blocks it has, and which elevation models and steps have run. -->

Pulau Tebing Tinggi is a *KHG* (*Kesatuan Hidrologis Gambut*, peat hydrological unit) in Riau. It covers 1,380 km², of which 1,364 km² is peat. It ranks 204 of 476 ranked KHG nationally (medium priority) with the default weights proposed for agreement with KLH and the World Bank, because little of its peat has burned: the burned share is below 0.4 % of peat per year, which still places it at the 68th percentile because 217 of the 476 ranked KHG have no burned peat at all (chapter 6). The mapped network has 96 km of primary, 408 km of secondary and 836 km of tertiary canals, and the national layer records 382 water-management structures, 168 of them realised canal blocks, none with a build year (chapter 3). No LiDAR covers this KHG, so the chain ran on corrected DeltaDTM and corrected DEMNAS only. Six SiTMAT water-table stations lie inside the KHG, and a station deficit was computed from five of them.

### Terrain and the three elevation models

{{figure: khg_dem_maps:14.10.03 | Pulau Tebing Tinggi: ground elevation from each elevation model on one colour scale, and their differences. Source: LiDAR terrain model, DeltaDTM and DEMNAS after the DEM correction | made by khg_dem_maps in re-peat/tools/project_report_khg.py from the KHG work folder}}

<!-- Writing prompt: Describe the terrain (dome, river levees, coast) and where the models disagree, for example along rivers, plantations or forest edges, and the likely reason (canopy left in the surface, coarse resolution, datum). -->

Both models show higher ground in the west and centre and a low corridor through the north-centre of the KHG, with the land falling towards the narrow south-eastern tail. They disagree over large blocks in the west and centre, where DEMNAS stands above DeltaDTM, and along strips in the north and south-east, where DeltaDTM is higher. DEMNAS is more than 2 m above DeltaDTM over 17 % of the KHG and more than 2 m below it over 5 %; the histogram of the difference (DeltaDTM minus DEMNAS) peaks at +0.87 m and its 5th to 95th percentile is -3.9 to +1.9 m. The blocky patches have the shape of canopy or land cover left in a surface model, and DEMNAS errors are largest under tree cover at the benchmark KHG (chapter 4), so canopy is the likely cause. Against the 2015 field survey elevations, DeltaDTM sits a median 0.37 m lower and DEMNAS 1.86 m higher (chapter 8).

<!-- Writing prompt: Compare relief and the median and spread of the difference to LiDAR; say whether the difference is a constant offset (datum) or local error, and what that means for the slopes the later steps use. -->

DeltaDTM has 30 m cells and covers 98 % of the KHG; DEMNAS has 8 m cells and covers all of it. Relief between the 5th and 95th percentiles is 2.4 to 10.2 m on DeltaDTM and 1.8 to 13.1 m on DEMNAS, so DEMNAS shows 11.3 m of relief against 7.8 m. The upper end of DeltaDTM lies at the 10 m limit of the land that product describes, so the highest ground is outside its scope (chapter 4). There is no LiDAR here, so the offset and local error of each model cannot be measured. At the two benchmark KHG, DeltaDTM differed from LiDAR mostly by a constant offset and DEMNAS by a larger local error (chapter 4), so the slopes used later are likely to be better on DeltaDTM, and block counts should be read as a range between the two models.

### Sub-KHG units

{{figure: khg_units_maps:14.10.03 | Pulau Tebing Tinggi: sub-KHG units delineated on each elevation model. Source: sub-KHG delineation on each elevation model, mapped canals | made by khg_units_maps in re-peat/tools/project_report_khg.py from the KHG work folder}}

<!-- Writing prompt: How many units each model gives, how their boundaries follow canals and rivers, and where the models draw them differently and why. -->

DeltaDTM and DEMNAS each give 29 *sub-KHG* units, with a median area of 45 km². The boundaries follow the mapped primary and secondary canals, which form a dense grid in the south-east and centre. DEMNAS draws a wider range of unit sizes, 21 to 126 km² against 24 to 90 km² on DeltaDTM, mainly in the west and north where few canals are mapped and terrain divides decide the lines.

The two unit sets share 73 % of the area in best-matching units, with an adjusted Rand index of 0.57. This is close to the value of 0.601 that the stability test of chapter 5 found for a flat surface with only the mapped canals, so the canal network sets most of the layout here and the terrain adds little. In the three highest-ranked sub-KHG the *sekat kanal* (canal block) step proposes 114 blocks on DeltaDTM and 127 on DEMNAS, and keeps 52 and 40 of them for 90 % of the water-level rise; the other 62 and 87 add little and are dropped. Blocks go on the analysed canals of the central units, where the surface falls along the canal, with one separate unit in the west on each model. The selected units are S13, S12 and S18 on DeltaDTM and S14, S19 and S12 on DEMNAS, so only S12 is shared. The analysed canals hold 48 existing blocks on DeltaDTM and 29 on DEMNAS, which shows that the two plans work on different canals.

DeltaDTM is the model to use for decisions here, because at the benchmark KHG its error over 1 km was 0.47 to 0.64 m against 1.66 to 2.49 m for DEMNAS (chapter 4), and DEMNAS block counts should not be taken as a design (chapter 9). ### Summary for this KHG

<!-- Writing prompt: Three to five sentences: the main finding for rewetting in this KHG, the recommended elevation model, the proposed blocks and budget, and what is still missing (data or steps not yet run). -->

Pulau Tebing Tinggi is a densely canalled KHG with little burned peat, and in the 2026 dry season its five stations show no deficit, so the case for rewetting here rests on its canals and existing blocks and not on fire or water loss. On DeltaDTM, the model to use, the plan keeps 52 canal blocks in the three highest-ranked sub-KHG, holding 2.43 million m³, for a central budget of 4 billion Rp. The choice of elevation model changes which sub-KHG are selected and with them the blocks and water held, while the balance of the KHG, the high-need area and the budget stay close. Pumping was not assessed, no LiDAR exists to measure the error of either model, the order of units by climatic balance is not usable, and the 2015 field survey could not test the water-table pattern (chapter 8).
