<!-- Writing prompt: Where the KHG lies, how much of it is peat, how it ranks nationally and why, what canal network and blocks it has, and which elevation models and steps have run. -->

Kahayan-Sebangau is a *KHG* (*Kesatuan Hidrologis Gambut*, peat hydrological unit) in Kalimantan Tengah and the site of the team's 2021 study. It covers 4,503 km², of which 3,955 km² is peat. It ranks 7 of 476 ranked KHG nationally (high priority) with the default weights proposed for agreement with KLH and the World Bank, and stays in the top 7 when only fire history is weighed, but falls to 75 when only canal indicators count (chapter 6). The 2015 fires burned 38.9 % of its peat and 2019 another 17.6 %; this year 2,538 hotspots fell on peat, and licence areas hold only 3.5 % of the peat, so most of the burning is in open land (chapter 6). The mapped network has 1,333 km of primary, 259 km of secondary and 7,980 km of tertiary canals, the national layer lists 1,333 water-management structures, and none of them is recorded as a realised canal block. No LiDAR covers this KHG and only one SiTMAT water-table station lies inside it, so the chain ran on two elevation models, corrected DeltaDTM and corrected DEMNAS. Every step ran on both except pumping, which was not assessed; the station deficit could be computed, but from one station.

### Terrain and the three elevation models

{{figure: khg_dem_maps:62.11-14.02 | Kahayan-Sebangau: ground elevation from each elevation model on one colour scale, and their differences. Source: LiDAR terrain model, DeltaDTM and DEMNAS after the DEM correction | made by khg_dem_maps in re-peat/tools/project_report_khg.py from the KHG work folder}}

<!-- Writing prompt: Describe the terrain (dome, river levees, coast) and where the models disagree, for example along rivers, plantations or forest edges, and the likely reason (canopy left in the surface, coarse resolution, datum). -->

With no LiDAR, the terrain can only be read from the two open models. The KHG is long and narrow, running from a northern tip down to a wide southern part. DeltaDTM shows low ground along the southern coast and the eastern margin, a broad ridge along the centre and a high at the northern tip. DEMNAS agrees on the broad pattern but is rougher and has dark patches in the north and along the centre that DeltaDTM does not show. DeltaDTM is higher than DEMNAS over the central ridge and lower in the west, the south-west and a large patch in the north, so the difference between them is not one offset: DeltaDTM is more than 2 m above DEMNAS over 11 % of the KHG and more than 2 m below it over 15 %. The likely causes are canopy and other surface features left in DEMNAS after correction (chapter 4 found its errors largest under tree cover), its 8 m cells against 30 m for DeltaDTM, and a datum offset that changes from place to place, since the raw DEMNAS offset to the ground was -3.4 m at Sebangau, -0.03 m at Mendahara and +2.16 m at Bengkalis (chapter 4).

<!-- Writing prompt: Compare relief and the median and spread of the difference to LiDAR; say whether the difference is a constant offset (datum) or local error, and what that means for the slopes the later steps use. -->

Relief between the 5th and 95th percentiles is 1.5 to 6.8 m on DeltaDTM and 1.3 to 10.6 m on DEMNAS, so DEMNAS spans about 1.8 times the range (9.3 m against 5.3 m, a ratio of the two spans). Without LiDAR there is no difference to a reference, and the table cannot say which model is closer to the ground. The only measured error is from the two LiDAR KHG: over 1 km DeltaDTM is off by 0.47 to 0.64 m and corrected DEMNAS by 1.66 to 2.19 m (chapter 4), and an earlier ICESat-2 test over this KHG gave 2.14 m for calibrated DEMNAS over 1 to 2 km. A head drop between blocks is about 0.3 m, so on both models the slopes along canals carry error of the same size as the signal, and DEMNAS more than DeltaDTM.

### Sub-KHG units

{{figure: khg_units_maps:62.11-14.02 | Kahayan-Sebangau: sub-KHG units delineated on each elevation model. Source: sub-KHG delineation on each elevation model, mapped canals | made by khg_units_maps in re-peat/tools/project_report_khg.py from the KHG work folder}}

<!-- Writing prompt: How many units each model gives, how their boundaries follow canals and rivers, and where the models draw them differently and why. -->

DeltaDTM gives 29 *sub-KHG* units and DEMNAS 27, both with a median area of 131 km² (ranges 75 to 617 km² and 73 to 445 km²). A long boundary on the east side runs the length of the KHG on both maps, and the units east of it are narrow strips. The models differ mostly in the west and south-west and in the north, where DeltaDTM draws small units and one very large one (617 km²) and DEMNAS draws fewer, more even units. The 2021 study used 16 units (4,523 km² in total, smallest 35 km²); the smallest unit now is 75 km² on DeltaDTM and 73 km² on DEMNAS, and chapter 5 notes that the comparison with the 2021 layout has still to be made.

DeltaDTM and DEMNAS share 70 % of the area in best-matching units, with an adjusted Rand index of 0.46. This is lower than the 0.60 that a flat surface gives at Sugihan-Lumpur with the mapped canals alone (chapter 5), so here the terrain moves the divides more than it does at that KHG. With two models there is one pair only, and a third set, LiDAR, does not exist at this KHG.

### Water balance and deficit

{{table: khg_water:62.11-14.02 | Kahayan-Sebangau: dry-season deficit from water-table stations and climatic balance per sub-KHG. Source: SiTMAT water-table stations, CHIRPS rain, MOD16 and WaPOR evapotranspiration | made by khg_water in re-peat/tools/project_report_khg.py from the KHG work folder}}

<!-- Writing prompt: State the deficit and its period, the months in deficit, and whether the order of units by balance is usable for each model; explain any model whose order test fails. -->

From 1 June to 15 October 2026 the station deficit is 348 mm (344 to 352 mm for a specific yield of 0.10 to 0.20), or 1,571 million m³ over the KHG, with 5 months in deficit in that window. It rests on one station, so it is low confidence and should be read as indicative; it does not depend on the elevation model. The order of units by climatic balance fails the test on both models: the two evapotranspiration products do not agree on the order of units, so the balance is shown but not used to rank units. In the 2021 units the mean balance followed forest share (correlation -0.82) while rain minus evapotranspiration did not (0.11, chapter 8), and the same interception effect probably sets the order here.

{{figure: khg_water_fig:62.11-14.02 | Kahayan-Sebangau: climatic balance through the mean year, and the balance of each sub-KHG, by elevation model. Source: CHIRPS rain and MOD16 evapotranspiration, 2018-2025 | made by khg_water_fig in re-peat/tools/project_report_khg.py from the KHG work folder}}

<!-- Writing prompt: Name the dry months, and say whether the elevation model changes the balance (it enters only through the units). -->

The mean climatic balance is negative in July, August and September and positive again in October; the wettest months are December to March. The KHG mean is 64.9 mm/month on DeltaDTM units and 64.8 on DEMNAS units (87.6 mm/month with WaPOR on both), and the deficit months per year are 2.88 and 2.89, so the elevation model does not change the balance of the KHG. It changes only the spread between units, 54.9 to 83.8 mm/month on DeltaDTM and 50.4 to 82.7 on DEMNAS, because each unit set averages the same rain and evapotranspiration grids over different areas.

### Priority of sub-KHG

{{figure: khg_priority_maps:62.11-14.02 | Kahayan-Sebangau: need for intervention per sub-KHG, by elevation model. Source: sub-KHG prioritisation with default weights | made by khg_priority_maps in re-peat/tools/project_report_khg.py from the KHG work folder}}

<!-- Writing prompt: Where the high-need units are and what drives them (fire, canals, dryness), and whether the same places come out on top with every model. Mention that the weights are the defaults proposed for agreement with KLH and the World Bank. -->

With the default weights proposed for agreement with KLH and the World Bank, high-need units form a block across the south-west and centre on both models, and a strip along the east. The three top-ranked units of both models lie in the south-centre, but they are different units. DEMNAS also puts the northern tip in high need, which DeltaDTM rates medium. The need score rests on fire history, dryness, canal and forest indicators (chapter 7); peat depth has no weight because there is no modelled depth layer for this KHG (chapter 3), and the water balance is left out because its order test fails. The burned share of 38.9 % in 2015 and 17.6 % in 2019 is the main reason for the high national rank.

{{table: khg_priority_agreement:62.11-14.02 | Kahayan-Sebangau: high-need area and agreement of the priority classes between elevation models. Source: sub-KHG prioritisation with default weights | made by khg_priority_agreement in re-peat/tools/project_report_khg.py from the KHG work folder}}

<!-- Writing prompt: Say how much area keeps its class across models and how strongly the scores agree; explain the largest difference. -->

High-need area is 1,843 km² (10 units) on DeltaDTM and 1,778 km² (9 units) on DEMNAS, about 41 % and 39 % of the KHG (ratios to 4,503 km²). 64 % of the area keeps its need class between the two models, 66 % of the high-need area in one is also high need in the other, and the area-weighted Spearman correlation of the scores is 0.66. The largest differences are at the northern tip and in the west, where each model draws units of different shape around the same fire and canal data, so a large unit that straddles burned and unburned land can change class when its boundary moves.

### Canal blocks and pumping

{{figure: khg_blocks_maps:62.11-14.02 | Kahayan-Sebangau: proposed canal blocks in the three highest-ranked sub-KHG, by elevation model. Source: RE-PEAT Interventions on each elevation model, mapped canals and existing blocks | made by khg_blocks_maps in re-peat/tools/project_report_khg.py from the KHG work folder}}

<!-- Writing prompt: Where the blocks go and why there (canal slope, burned land), how many are dropped as low benefit, and how the selected units and block positions change with the model. -->

In the three highest-ranked sub-KHG the *sekat kanal* (canal block) step proposes 30 blocks on DeltaDTM and 21 on DEMNAS, keeps 13 and 7 for 90 % of the water-level rise, and drops the other 17 and 14 as low benefit. Blocks lie on the canals that form or cross the unit edges, in rows along the east-west canals on DeltaDTM and along the western and northern canals on DEMNAS. The selected units differ (S24, S15 and S21 on DeltaDTM; S12, S22 and S18 on DEMNAS), and the south-western unit chosen on DEMNAS has no block on any analysed canal. There is no existing block in the KHG to build on, and the slope rule of the ranking asks for 3,276 blocks on DeltaDTM and 7,466 on DEMNAS over the whole KHG, far more than the plan keeps.

{{table: khg_interventions:62.11-14.02 | Kahayan-Sebangau: canal blocks, water held and budget, by elevation model. Source: RE-PEAT Interventions and sub-KHG prioritisation on each elevation model | made by khg_interventions in re-peat/tools/project_report_khg.py from the KHG work folder}}

<!-- Writing prompt: Give the blocks proposed and kept, water held against the deficit, and the central budget with its main cost item; say what drives the cost differences between models. -->

The kept blocks hold 2.69 million m³ on DeltaDTM and 1.51 million m³ on DEMNAS, against a deficit of the selected units of 127.1 and 111.7 million m³, or 2.1 % and 1.4 % of it. The central budget is 95 billion Rp on DeltaDTM (56 low, 111 high) and 241 billion Rp on DEMNAS (152 low, 283 high). Revegetation of burned land is the main item, 66 of 95 and 182 of 241 billion Rp. The 2.5-fold difference in budget therefore comes mostly from the units each model selects and the burned land in them, and little from the number of blocks. Pumping was not assessed.

### How the elevation model changes the result

{{table: khg_dem_effect:62.11-14.02 | Kahayan-Sebangau: headline outputs by elevation model and their spread. Source: RE-PEAT results on each elevation model | made by khg_dem_effect in re-peat/tools/project_report_khg.py from the KHG work folder}}

<!-- Writing prompt: Which outputs move most with the elevation model and which barely move; for this KHG, which model you would use for decisions and why. -->

The balance of the KHG does not move (64.9 and 64.8 mm/month), and the number of units, the median unit area and the high-need area change by a factor of 1.1 or less. The outputs that depend on canal slopes and on the choice of units move most: blocks needed by the slope rule by ×2.3, blocks proposed by ×1.4, blocks kept by ×1.9, water held by ×1.8 and the central budget by ×2.5. The three selected units differ between the models, so the spread is not terrain alone.

Neither model can be checked here, because no LiDAR exists. DeltaDTM is the better choice for decisions: at the two LiDAR KHG its error over 1 km was 0.47 to 0.64 m against 1.66 to 2.19 m for DEMNAS (chapter 4), and its relief of 1.5 to 6.8 m at the 5th to 95th percentile sits inside the 10 m limit of the product. The block counts should be read as a range between the two models, 7 to 13 kept blocks, and the DEMNAS count should not be taken as a design (chapter 9).

### Summary for this KHG

<!-- Writing prompt: Three to five sentences: the main finding for rewetting in this KHG, the recommended elevation model, the proposed blocks and budget, and what is still missing (data or steps not yet run). -->

Kahayan-Sebangau is a heavily burned KHG, mostly outside licence areas, with a dry season from July to September and a station deficit of 348 mm (1,571 million m³) that rests on one station and is indicative only. On DeltaDTM, the model to prefer without a LiDAR check, the plan keeps 13 canal blocks in the three highest-ranked sub-KHG; they hold 2.69 million m³, about 2 % of the deficit of those units, so blocking cannot rewet this KHG alone, and the central budget is 95 billion Rp, mostly revegetation of burned land. The choice of model changes which sub-KHG are selected, so kept blocks (13 and 7) and budget (95 and 241 billion Rp) move by ×1.9 and ×2.5, while the KHG balance and the high-need area stay almost the same. Still missing are LiDAR or levelling to measure the terrain error, a peat depth layer, more water-table stations, the pumping step, and a comparison with the 16 units of the 2021 study.
