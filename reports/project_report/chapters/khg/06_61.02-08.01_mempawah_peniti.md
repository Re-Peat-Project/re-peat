<!-- Writing prompt: Where the KHG lies, how much of it is peat, how it ranks nationally and why, what canal network and blocks it has, and which elevation models and steps have run. -->

Mempawah-Peniti is a *KHG* (*Kesatuan Hidrologis Gambut*, peat hydrological unit) in Kalimantan Barat. It covers 510 km², of which 376 km² is peat. It ranks 21 of 476 ranked KHG nationally (high priority) with the default weights proposed for agreement with KLH and the World Bank, and rises to 10 when this year's hotspots are weighed more, to 6 when canals are weighed more and to 3 with equal weights (chapter 6). It is at the 98th percentile for this year's hotspots and for secondary and tertiary canal density, and 6.0 % of its peat has burned in the part of 2026 covered so far, more than in any full year since 2015. None of its 691 hotspots on peat this year fell inside a licence area. The mapped network has 212 km of primary, 1,282 km of secondary and 3,422 km of tertiary canals, and the national layer records 264 water-management structures but no canal block recorded as realised. No LiDAR covers the KHG and no SiTMAT water-table station lies inside it, so the chain ran on corrected DeltaDTM and corrected DEMNAS only and no station deficit could be computed.

### Terrain and the three elevation models

{{figure: khg_dem_maps:61.02-08.01 | Mempawah-Peniti: ground elevation from each elevation model on one colour scale, and their differences. Source: LiDAR terrain model, DeltaDTM and DEMNAS after the DEM correction | made by khg_dem_maps in re-peat/tools/project_report_khg.py from the KHG work folder}}

<!-- Writing prompt: Describe the terrain (dome, river levees, coast) and where the models disagree, for example along rivers, plantations or forest edges, and the likely reason (canopy left in the surface, coarse resolution, datum). -->

Both models show a low coastal plain in the south-west that rises towards the north and east, but they disagree on how high. DeltaDTM shows a gentle high in the north and a broad rise in the east, while DEMNAS shows the north and the east-centre as large dark blocks and low values along the south-west coast. The difference map has two large areas, one in the north and one in the east, where DEMNAS stands well above DeltaDTM, and a band across the west and centre where DeltaDTM is higher. DEMNAS is more than 2 m above DeltaDTM over 31 % of the KHG and more than 2 m below it over 33 %. The blocky outlines of the northern and eastern patches suggest surface cover left in DEMNAS after the correction (chapter 4 found its errors largest under tree cover and cropland at Sugihan-Lumpur), but with no LiDAR here this cannot be confirmed. DeltaDTM is defined only for land below 10 m above mean sea level (chapter 4), so the highest ground of this KHG lies at the edge of its range.

<!-- Writing prompt: Compare relief and the median and spread of the difference to LiDAR; say whether the difference is a constant offset (datum) or local error, and what that means for the slopes the later steps use. -->

Relief between the 5th and 95th percentiles is 1.5 to 10.4 m on DeltaDTM (30 m cells) and -2.2 to 16.6 m on DEMNAS (8 m cells), a span of 8.9 m against 18.8 m. With no LiDAR the difference to LiDAR cannot be given. The histogram of DeltaDTM minus DEMNAS peaks at +2.80 m and is broad, with a 5th percentile of -8.8 m. A constant datum offset would give a narrow peak, so much of the difference is local and changes the slopes along canals, which the canal-block step uses. Over 1 km, the error against LiDAR was 0.47 to 0.64 m for DeltaDTM and between 1.66 and 2.49 m for DEMNAS, FABDEM and GLO-30 at the two LiDAR KHG (chapter 4), so block counts here should be read as a range between the two models.

DeltaDTM gives 28 *sub-KHG* units and DEMNAS 29. The dense canal grid, with long straight canals running north to south in the east, sets many boundaries in both maps (chapter 5). ### Water balance and deficit

{{table: khg_water:61.02-08.01 | Mempawah-Peniti: dry-season deficit from water-table stations and climatic balance per sub-KHG. <!-- Writing prompt: State the deficit and its period, the months in deficit, and whether the order of units by balance is usable for each model; explain any model whose order test fails. -->

No dry-season deficit from stations is given, because no water-table station lies inside the KHG. The climatic balance (CHIRPS rain minus evapotranspiration, 2018 to 2025) averages 62.9 mm/month over the DeltaDTM units and 63.0 mm/month over the DEMNAS units, with 2.40 deficit months per year on both. The order of units by balance is not usable with either model, because the two evapotranspiration products do not agree on the order of the units (chapter 8); they also differ in level, with WaPOR at 101.4 mm/month on both unit sets against 62.9 and 63.0 with MOD16. The unit balances (55.2 to 77.4 mm/month on DeltaDTM, 55.9 to 74.6 on DEMNAS) are therefore shown as a range and not used as a ranking.

<!-- Writing prompt: Name the dry months, and say whether the elevation model changes the balance (it enters only through the units). -->

In the mean year the balance falls to 0 mm/month in July and 6 mm/month in February, and stays low in August and September (35 and 27 mm/month). October to January are the wet months, at 108 to 132 mm/month. The elevation model changes the KHG balance by only 0.1 mm/month, because it enters only through the unit boundaries, which average the same rain and evapotranspiration grids over different areas.

With the default weights proposed for agreement with KLH and the World Bank, high-need units form a block in the north and centre-north, a row of long narrow units east of the centre and a group near the south-east coast, while the south-west coast is low need on both models. The first-ranked unit lies in the north on both models. The second and third lie in the west-centre and the centre-north on DeltaDTM, and in the centre and the centre-north on DEMNAS. The rank of the KHG rises when this year's hotspots or canals weigh more (chapter 6), which fits the dense network and the 2026 burn.

High-need area is 229 km² (10 units) on DeltaDTM and 232 km² (10 units) on DEMNAS. 82 % of the overlap keeps its need class, 83 % of the high-need area in DeltaDTM is also high need in DEMNAS, and the area-weighted Spearman of the scores is 0.83. This is higher than the 57 to 65 % at Sugihan-Lumpur. The differences come from the units, since each model groups the same fire, canal and dryness data into different shapes, and the top three units are not the same units on the two models.

### Canal blocks and pumping

{{figure: khg_blocks_maps:61.02-08.01 | Mempawah-Peniti: proposed canal blocks in the three highest-ranked sub-KHG, by elevation model. Source: RE-PEAT Interventions on each elevation model, mapped canals and existing blocks | made by khg_blocks_maps in re-peat/tools/project_report_khg.py from the KHG work folder}}

<!-- Writing prompt: Where the blocks go and why there (canal slope, burned land), how many are dropped as low benefit, and how the selected units and block positions change with the model. -->

The three selected sub-KHG differ between the models: S03, S07 and S08 on DeltaDTM, S01, S12 and S06 on DEMNAS. DeltaDTM puts its blocks in the north and the west-centre, while the DEMNAS blocks follow a long strip from the north towards the south. The head rule proposes 217 blocks on DeltaDTM and 282 on DEMNAS (×1.3), and the 90 % balance then keeps 117 of the 217 (54 %) on DeltaDTM but only 27 of the 282 (10 %) on DEMNAS. The larger height steps along DEMNAS canals probably put most of the water-level rise into a few blocks, so the other 255 add little and are dropped. On DeltaDTM the rise is spread over more blocks. No existing block is counted on any analysed canal, so every kept block is new.

{{table: khg_interventions:61.02-08.01 | Mempawah-Peniti: canal blocks, water held and budget, by elevation model. Source: RE-PEAT Interventions and sub-KHG prioritisation on each elevation model | made by khg_interventions in re-peat/tools/project_report_khg.py from the KHG work folder}}

<!-- Writing prompt: Give the blocks proposed and kept, water held against the deficit, and the central budget with its main cost item; say what drives the cost differences between models. -->

Kept blocks hold 2.18 million m³ on DeltaDTM and 6.97 million m³ on DEMNAS (×3.2), or 0.019 and 0.26 million m³ per kept block, a ratio of about 14. The deficit of the selected units is not available without a station deficit, so the water held is not compared with it. The central budget is 44 billion Rp on DeltaDTM (30 low) and 158 billion Rp on DEMNAS (105 low). Revegetation of burned land is 1 billion Rp of the DEMNAS total and is not shown for DeltaDTM, so it is not the main item, and the table does not itemise the rest (chapter 9 lists the budget items; blocks outside the selected units are priced from an estimate of missing blocks). The budget ratio of ×3.6 is close to the ×4.0 ratio in blocks needed by the slope rule over the whole KHG (4,586 on DeltaDTM, 18,544 on DEMNAS) and moves opposite to the blocks kept. Pumping was not assessed.

### How the elevation model changes the result

{{table: khg_dem_effect:61.02-08.01 | Mempawah-Peniti: headline outputs by elevation model and their spread. Source: RE-PEAT results on each elevation model | made by khg_dem_effect in re-peat/tools/project_report_khg.py from the KHG work folder}}

<!-- Writing prompt: Which outputs move most with the elevation model and which barely move; for this KHG, which model you would use for decisions and why. -->

The KHG balance (62.9 and 63.0 mm/month), the number of units (28 and 29) and the high-need area (229 and 232 km², 10 units on both) barely move. Outputs that depend on canal slopes move most: blocks needed by the slope rule over the whole KHG by ×4.0, blocks kept by ×4.3 (more on DeltaDTM), water held by ×3.2 and the central budget by ×3.6. Blocks, water and budget refer to the three selected sub-KHG, which differ between models, so they move with the choice of units as well as with the terrain.

With no LiDAR there is no measured error for either model here. Chapter 4 recommends processed DeltaDTM as the closer model for a KHG without LiDAR, with a measured error of 0.47 to 0.64 m over 1 km, so DeltaDTM is the model to use for decisions, with DEMNAS read as the upper end of the range for water held and cost. DEMNAS plans a few high-benefit blocks on a surface that probably carries canopy and cropland in the north and east, and chapter 9 concludes that DEMNAS block counts should not be taken as a design.

### Summary for this KHG

<!-- Writing prompt: Three to five sentences: the main finding for rewetting in this KHG, the recommended elevation model, the proposed blocks and budget, and what is still missing (data or steps not yet run). -->

Mempawah-Peniti is a high-priority KHG (rank 21 of 476) with a dense canal network and a large share of its peat burned this year. The two models agree on the climatic balance (about 63 mm/month, 2.40 deficit months a year), on the high-need area (229 to 232 km², 10 units) and broadly on where the high-need units lie (82 % of the area keeps its class). They disagree on the plan: DeltaDTM keeps 117 blocks holding 2.18 million m³ for a central budget of 44 billion Rp, and DEMNAS keeps 27 blocks holding 6.97 million m³ for 158 billion Rp, in different sub-KHG, so block counts should be read as a range between the two and DeltaDTM used for decisions. Still missing are LiDAR or a levelled reference to measure the error here, a water-table station to give the station deficit, a usable order of units by balance, and pumping, which was not assessed.
