<!-- Writing prompt: Where the KHG lies, how much of it is peat, how it ranks nationally and why, what canal network and blocks it has, and which elevation models and steps have run. -->

Balangan-Batangalai is a *KHG* (*Kesatuan Hidrologis Gambut*, peat hydrological unit) in Kalimantan Selatan and one of the smallest of the selected KHG, with 308 km², of which 137 km² is peat. It ranks 4 of 476 ranked KHG nationally (high priority) with the default weights proposed for agreement with KLH and the World Bank. It is in the top 7 at default weights and when only fire history is weighed, but falls to 54 when only this year's hotspots count (chapter 6). The mapped network has 157 km of primary, 395 km of secondary and 827 km of tertiary canals, and the national layer records 112 water-management structures, none of them a realised canal block. No LiDAR covers the KHG, so the chain ran on corrected DeltaDTM and corrected DEMNAS only. No SiTMAT water-table station lies inside the KHG, so no station-based deficit can be computed, and pumping was not assessed.

### Terrain and the three elevation models

{{figure: khg_dem_maps:63.07-08-11.01 | Balangan-Batangalai: ground elevation from each elevation model on one colour scale, and their differences. <!-- Writing prompt: Describe the terrain (dome, river levees, coast) and where the models disagree, for example along rivers, plantations or forest edges, and the likely reason (canopy left in the surface, coarse resolution, datum). -->

Both models show the same terrain: the ground is lowest in the south-west and rises along the north-east and east edges; elevation between the 5th and 95th percentiles is 3.1 to 10.0 m on DeltaDTM and 2.6 to 12.1 m on DEMNAS. DeltaDTM is smooth. DEMNAS has the same trend with a blotchy texture at the scale of a few hundred metres. In the difference map DEMNAS stands higher than DeltaDTM along the northern and eastern edges and in strips in the south-east, and lower in patches in the south-west. 87 % of the cells differ by less than 2 m and the histogram peaks at -0.45 m (DeltaDTM minus DEMNAS). Patches of this kind are what a surface model gives where vegetation or buildings remain in the heights after correction (chapter 4).

<!-- Writing prompt: Compare relief and the median and spread of the difference to LiDAR; say whether the difference is a constant offset (datum) or local error, and what that means for the slopes the later steps use. -->

Relief between the 5th and 95th percentiles is 3.1 to 10.0 m (6.9 m) on DeltaDTM and 2.6 to 12.1 m (9.5 m) on DEMNAS, so DEMNAS shows 2.6 m more relief over the same ground. DeltaDTM covers 98 % of the KHG at 30 m and DEMNAS 100 % at 8 m. With no LiDAR here, neither model can be measured against a reference. At the two KHG that have LiDAR, corrected DeltaDTM had a height-difference error of 0.47 and 0.64 m over 1 km and DEMNAS 1.66 to 2.49 m, while the real 1 km relief of the LiDAR was 0.38 and 0.46 m (chapter 4). If the same holds here, part of the extra DEMNAS relief is error, so DEMNAS slopes and block counts should be read as the upper end of a range with DeltaDTM at the lower end.

### Sub-KHG units

{{figure: khg_units_maps:63.07-08-11.01 | Balangan-Batangalai: sub-KHG units delineated on each elevation model. Source: sub-KHG delineation on each elevation model, mapped canals | made by khg_units_maps in re-peat/tools/project_report_khg.py from the KHG work folder}}

<!-- Writing prompt: How many units each model gives, how their boundaries follow canals and rivers, and where the models draw them differently and why. -->

DeltaDTM and DEMNAS each give 22 *sub-KHG* units, with median areas of 11 and 12 km². The count is below the cap of about 30 because the automatic minimum area (chapter 5) did not need to rise: it started at 5 km², and merging the 2,077 pieces on DeltaDTM and 2,264 on DEMNAS at that size already gave 22 units. Units range from 6 to 48 km² on DeltaDTM and from 7 to 56 km² on DEMNAS. Boundaries follow the dense canal network in the centre and south-west, with 5,862 mapped canal segments in 308 km², and no river layer is mapped for this KHG (chapter 3). The north-east and east have few canals and hold the largest units.

The two unit sets share 71 % of the area in best-matching units, with an adjusted Rand index of 0.54. At Sugihan-Lumpur the three pairs scored 0.60 to 0.66, close to the value for a flat surface with canals only (0.601, chapter 5). No water-table station lies inside the KHG, so no dry-season deficit from stations is computed and no deficit period can be stated from observations. The climatic balance per sub-KHG (chapter 8) has a mean of 64.8 mm/month on DeltaDTM units and 64.9 mm/month on DEMNAS units, with 2.47 and 2.48 deficit months a year; this is within the 58 to 84 mm/month and 2.2 to 2.7 months found at the selected KHG. The order of units by balance is not usable on either model, because the two evapotranspiration products do not agree on that order. Unit values range from 41.5 to 80.9 mm/month on DeltaDTM and from 40.2 to 83.1 mm/month on DEMNAS, but that spread should not be used to rank units.

The mean-year balance is negative in August and September, lowest in September at -22 mm/month, and positive again in October. ### Priority of sub-KHG

<!-- Writing prompt: Where the high-need units are and what drives them (fire, canals, dryness), and whether the same places come out on top with every model. Mention that the weights are the defaults proposed for agreement with KLH and the World Bank. -->

With the default weights proposed for agreement with KLH and the World Bank, 8 units (116 km²) are high need on DeltaDTM and 8 units (143 km²) on DEMNAS. On both models the top-ranked unit lies in the south-west centre, where the canals are densest. The second and third differ: DeltaDTM places them in the south-west centre and in a large unit in the north-east, and DEMNAS in the north and in the south centre. The need score combines fire, vegetation dryness, canals and peat depth (chapter 7). The water balance cannot enter because its order test failed, and modelled peat depth covers only 53 % of this KHG, the lowest of the selected KHG that have it (chapter 3), so its weight still needs a decision.

In the three highest-ranked sub-KHG the *sekat kanal* (canal block) step proposes 18 blocks on DeltaDTM and keeps 11, and proposes 59 on DEMNAS and keeps 23; 7 and 36 are dropped as adding little. Blocks sit along canals in the south-west and, on DeltaDTM, in the north-centre. The selected units differ: S18, S17 and S04 on DeltaDTM and S18, S03 and S19 on DEMNAS, and only S18 is shared. DEMNAS puts most of its blocks in a dense cluster in the south-west, which follows local noise in its surface as at Sugihan-Lumpur (chapter 9). With no LiDAR to measure against, the block counts should be read as a range between the two models.

### How the elevation model changes the result

{{table: khg_dem_effect:63.07-08-11.01 | Balangan-Batangalai: headline outputs by elevation model and their spread. Source: RE-PEAT results on each elevation model | made by khg_dem_effect in re-peat/tools/project_report_khg.py from the KHG work folder}}

<!-- Writing prompt: Which outputs move most with the elevation model and which barely move; for this KHG, which model you would use for decisions and why. -->

The KHG balance barely moves (0.1 mm/month), and the number of units, the median unit area, the area covered and the number of high-need units move by a factor of 1.1 or less. The high-need area moves by ×1.2. The outputs that depend on canal slopes and on which units are selected move most: blocks needed by the slope rule over the whole KHG range from 723 on DeltaDTM to 2,277 on DEMNAS (×3.1), blocks proposed by the head rule by ×3.3, blocks kept by ×2.1, the central budget by ×3.0 and water held by ×6.6. The last three also move because the selected units change.

No LiDAR exists here, and the benchmark of chapter 4 found corrected DeltaDTM closer to LiDAR at the two sites that have it. DeltaDTM is therefore the model to use for decisions, with DEMNAS as the upper end of the range of block counts, and its error is not measured at this KHG.

### Summary for this KHG

<!-- Writing prompt: Three to five sentences: the main finding for rewetting in this KHG, the recommended elevation model, the proposed blocks and budget, and what is still missing (data or steps not yet run). -->

Balangan-Batangalai is a small, high-ranked KHG with a dense canal network in the south-west and no realised canal blocks. On DeltaDTM, the model recommended here, the plan keeps 11 canal blocks in the three highest-ranked sub-KHG, holding 0.15 million m³, for a central budget of 8 billion Rp; DEMNAS gives 23 blocks, 0.96 million m³ and 23 billion Rp. The KHG balance does not change with the model, but the selected units do, and with them blocks, water and budget; the two unit sets agree on 71 % of the area. Neither model can be checked against LiDAR here. No water-table station lies inside the KHG, so no deficit can be computed, the order of units by climatic balance is not usable, pumping was not assessed, and modelled peat depth covers only 53 % of the KHG.
