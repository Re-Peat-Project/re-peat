<!-- Writing prompt: Where the KHG lies, how much of it is peat, how it ranks nationally and why, what canal network and blocks it has, and which elevation models and steps have run. -->

Buru Mappi-Buru Obaa is a *KHG* (*Kesatuan Hidrologis Gambut*, peat hydrological unit) in Papua (Papua Selatan, chapter 3). It covers 3,979 km², of which 1,699 km² is peat. It ranks 138 of 476 ranked KHG nationally (high priority) with the default weights proposed for agreement with KLH and the World Bank, and 49 when only this year's hotspots are counted. Its burned share is low (0.7 % of peat a year), but 920 hotspots fell on its peat in 2026, 703 of them in July, which puts it 12th by this year's count (chapter 6). It has the least data of the main KHG: 5 km of primary, 11 km of secondary and 15 km of tertiary canal are mapped, and there are no water-management structures, no canal blocks, no water-table station, no modelled peat depth and no LiDAR. The chain therefore ran on corrected DeltaDTM and corrected DEMNAS only.


### Terrain and the three elevation models

{{figure: khg_dem_maps:91.18-17.01 | Buru Mappi-Buru Obaa: ground elevation from each elevation model on one colour scale, and their differences. Source: LiDAR terrain model, DeltaDTM and DEMNAS after the DEM correction | made by khg_dem_maps in re-peat/tools/project_report_khg.py from the KHG work folder}}

<!-- Writing prompt: Describe the terrain (dome, river levees, coast) and where the models disagree, for example along rivers, plantations or forest edges, and the likely reason (canopy left in the surface, coarse resolution, datum). -->

The KHG is a long, narrow strip running north to south, with a wider lobe in the north. Both models show higher ground along a central band, and lower ground in the northern lobe and at the southern tip. DEMNAS resolves a branching pattern of stream valleys inside the higher ground, while DeltaDTM, at 30 m, shows the same band as a smooth surface. The difference map has DEMNAS higher than DeltaDTM over much of the northern lobe and the south-west, and DeltaDTM higher in patches of the central band. DEMNAS is more than 2 m above DeltaDTM over 46 % of the KHG and more than 2 m below it over 18 %, and the 5th to 95th percentile of the difference is -10.1 to +4.0 m. The pattern follows forest cover and valleys more than a regular offset, which points to canopy left in DEMNAS in places and to smoothing of valleys in DeltaDTM. There is no LiDAR here, so neither model can be measured against a reference.

<!-- Writing prompt: Compare relief and the median and spread of the difference to LiDAR; say whether the difference is a constant offset (datum) or local error, and what that means for the slopes the later steps use. -->

Relief between the 5th and 95th percentiles is 3.1 to 21.2 m on DeltaDTM and 0.5 to 22.7 m on DEMNAS, which is 18.1 and 22.2 m. Both cover 99 % of the KHG. Without LiDAR no difference to a reference can be given. At the two KHG that have LiDAR, the corrected DEMNAS differed from it by an NMAD of 1.60 and 2.28 m and DeltaDTM by 0.64 and 0.46 m (chapter 4), so DEMNAS has the larger local error at both. Those sites are flat Sumatran peat plains and the same errors may not hold on this terrain, but they are the only guide. Slopes taken from DEMNAS should be treated as less certain than slopes from DeltaDTM.


### Sub-KHG units

{{figure: khg_units_maps:91.18-17.01 | Buru Mappi-Buru Obaa: sub-KHG units delineated on each elevation model. Source: sub-KHG delineation on each elevation model, mapped canals | made by khg_units_maps in re-peat/tools/project_report_khg.py from the KHG work folder}}

<!-- Writing prompt: How many units each model gives, how their boundaries follow canals and rivers, and where the models draw them differently and why. -->

DeltaDTM gives 30 *sub-KHG* units and DEMNAS 27. Only 16 km of primary and secondary canal is mapped, so the unit boundaries have few canals to follow. The maps show compact polygons that are smaller in the northern lobe and at the southern tip and larger in the central strip. The two sets differ mainly in the central strip, where DeltaDTM draws one unit of 845 km², and in the northern lobe, where the boundaries fall in different places.

The two unit sets share 70 % of the area in best-matching units, with an adjusted Rand index of 0.60. Median unit area is 88 km² on DeltaDTM (range 60 to 845 km²) and 126 km² on DEMNAS (range 70 to 666 km²). The index is close to the 0.601 that a flat surface gave at Sugihan-Lumpur in the stability test of chapter 5, although that test used a dense canal network. Here the elevation model changes where the units fall, and no LiDAR unit set exists to show which one is closer.


### Water balance and deficit

{{table: khg_water:91.18-17.01 | Buru Mappi-Buru Obaa: dry-season deficit from water-table stations and climatic balance per sub-KHG. Source: SiTMAT water-table stations, CHIRPS rain, MOD16 and WaPOR evapotranspiration | made by khg_water in re-peat/tools/project_report_khg.py from the KHG work folder}}

<!-- Writing prompt: State the deficit and its period, the months in deficit, and whether the order of units by balance is usable for each model; explain any model whose order test fails. -->

No water-table station lies inside the KHG, so no station-based dry-season deficit can be computed. The climatic balance per sub-KHG averages 164.8 mm/month with MOD16 evapotranspiration and 184.0 mm/month with WaPOR on both unit sets, and the deficit months average 0.59 a year on DeltaDTM units and 0.60 on DEMNAS units. The order of units by balance is not usable with either model, because the two evapotranspiration products do not agree on that order, so the balance values are left out of the unit ranking (chapter 8).

<!-- Writing prompt: Name the dry months, and say whether the elevation model changes the balance (it enters only through the units). -->

The mean balance stays positive in every month. It is lowest in February and again in August and September, and highest in May and November. The KHG mean is the same on both unit sets (164.8 mm/month), so the elevation model does not change it. It changes only the spread between units, from 154.8 to 172.1 mm/month on DeltaDTM units and 156.5 to 174.0 mm/month on DEMNAS units. This is far above the 58 to 84 mm/month of the Sumatran KHG in chapter 8, so the climatic balance here shows little seasonal shortage of water.


### Priority of sub-KHG

{{figure: khg_priority_maps:91.18-17.01 | Buru Mappi-Buru Obaa: need for intervention per sub-KHG, by elevation model. Source: sub-KHG prioritisation with default weights | made by khg_priority_maps in re-peat/tools/project_report_khg.py from the KHG work folder}}

<!-- Writing prompt: Where the high-need units are and what drives them (fire, canals, dryness), and whether the same places come out on top with every model. Mention that the weights are the defaults proposed for agreement with KLH and the World Bank. -->

With the default weights proposed for agreement with KLH and the World Bank, high-need units cover the central and southern strip on both models, and the north-western lobe has medium or high need depending on the model. The three top-ranked units lie at the southern tip on both. Peat depth has weight 0 because no depth layer exists (chapter 3), and the balance is left out because its order is not usable, so the score rests on the fire, vegetation dryness, canal and other indicators of chapter 7. This KHG has little burned area but many hotspots this year, so the ranking reflects current fire activity more than past burning.

High-need area is 2,124 km² (10 units) on DeltaDTM and 1,725 km² (9 units) on DEMNAS. 74 % of the area keeps its need class between the two models, 76 % of the high-need area in one is also high need in the other, and the area-weighted Spearman correlation of the scores is 0.81. The largest difference is in the north-western lobe, where DeltaDTM marks two units as high need and DEMNAS marks one.


### Canal blocks and pumping

{{figure: khg_blocks_maps:91.18-17.01 | Buru Mappi-Buru Obaa: proposed canal blocks in the three highest-ranked sub-KHG, by elevation model. Source: RE-PEAT Interventions on each elevation model, mapped canals and existing blocks | made by khg_blocks_maps in re-peat/tools/project_report_khg.py from the KHG work folder}}

<!-- Writing prompt: Where the blocks go and why there (canal slope, burned land), how many are dropped as low benefit, and how the selected units and block positions change with the model. -->

The three selected sub-KHG are S30, S29 and S28 on DeltaDTM and S27, S25 and S26 on DEMNAS. The labels differ because each model has its own unit set, but the units lie at the same southern tip of the KHG. The *sekat kanal* (canal block) step proposes 18 blocks on DeltaDTM and 34 on DEMNAS, and keeps 13 and 20 for 90 % of the water-level rise; the other 5 and 14 add little and are dropped. The blocks cluster along the few mapped canals at the eastern edge of the selected units, since the rest of the selected area has no mapped canal to block. No existing blocks are counted.

<!-- Writing prompt: Give the blocks proposed and kept, water held against the deficit, and the central budget with its main cost item; say what drives the cost differences between models. -->

The kept blocks hold 0.13 million m³ on DeltaDTM and 0.21 million m³ on DEMNAS. No deficit can be given for the selected units, since there is no station deficit, so the water held cannot be set against one. The central budget is 75 billion Rp on DeltaDTM (48 to 92 from low to high) and 15 billion Rp on DEMNAS (10 to 18), of which revegetation of burned land is 74 and 14 billion Rp. Both budgets follow the burned land inside the selected units, and the blocks themselves are a small part. Pumping was not assessed.


### How the elevation model changes the result

{{table: khg_dem_effect:91.18-17.01 | Buru Mappi-Buru Obaa: headline outputs by elevation model and their spread. Source: RE-PEAT results on each elevation model | made by khg_dem_effect in re-peat/tools/project_report_khg.py from the KHG work folder}}

<!-- Writing prompt: Which outputs move most with the elevation model and which barely move; for this KHG, which model you would use for decisions and why. -->

The KHG balance does not move (164.8 mm/month on both), and the number of units, the high-need area and the number of high-need units change by a factor of 1.2 or less. Blocks needed by the slope rule of the ranking over the whole KHG move most: 73 on DeltaDTM and 303 on DEMNAS (×4.2). The head rule gives ×1.9, kept blocks ×1.5, water held ×1.6 and the central budget ×5.0, the last mostly through the choice of units. The totals are small because the mapped network is short, 31 km in all. Per kilometre of mapped canal the slope rule gives 2.4 blocks on DeltaDTM and 9.8 on DEMNAS, against 0.26 and 1.8 at Sugihan-Lumpur (3,634 and 25,580 blocks over 14,231 km), so canal length explains the small totals while the density is not low. The relief here is also larger than at Sugihan-Lumpur, where it is 0.8 to 7.0 m on LiDAR.

With no LiDAR, block counts should be read as a range between the two models. DEMNAS counts are likely inflated by local error (chapter 9 advises against taking them as a design), and DeltaDTM had the smaller error at both LiDAR KHG. For decisions here DeltaDTM is the better choice, with the DEMNAS result as an upper bound, until a reference such as LiDAR or surveyed canal levels is available.


### Summary for this KHG

<!-- Writing prompt: Three to five sentences: the main finding for rewetting in this KHG, the recommended elevation model, the proposed blocks and budget, and what is still missing (data or steps not yet run). -->

Buru Mappi-Buru Obaa ranks 138 of 476 nationally, and the ranking rests on its 920 hotspots on peat in 2026 more than on past burning. The analysis has no LiDAR, water-table station or peat depth and only 31 km of mapped canal, so no station deficit can be computed and neither elevation model can be checked against a reference. On DeltaDTM, the model to use, the plan keeps 13 canal blocks in the three highest-ranked sub-KHG at the southern tip. They hold 0.13 million m³, and the central budget is 75 billion Rp, mostly revegetation of burned land. DEMNAS gives 20 blocks and 15 billion Rp for a different set of units, so the block counts should be read as a range between the two models. Pumping was not assessed.
