<!-- Writing prompt: Where the KHG lies, how much of it is peat, how it ranks nationally and why, what canal network and blocks it has, and which elevation models and steps have run. -->

Pulau Bengkalis is a *KHG* (*Kesatuan Hidrologis Gambut*, peat hydrological unit) in Riau that covers the island of Bengkalis. It covers 906 km², of which 893 km² is peat, and ranks 69 of 476 ranked KHG nationally (high priority) with the default weights proposed for agreement with KLH and the World Bank. This year's fire record stands out: 6.0 % of its peat has burned in the part of 2026 covered so far, more than in any full year since 2015, and 389 of its 674 hotspots on peat fell in February (chapter 6). The mapped network has 242 km of primary, 342 km of secondary and 1,281 km of tertiary canals, about 2.1 km of canal per km² (the three classes added and divided by the area), and the national layer records 205 water-management structures, 46 of them realised canal blocks. No LiDAR covers this KHG and only 2 SiTMAT water-table stations lie inside it. The chain therefore ran on corrected DeltaDTM and corrected DEMNAS only, with no reference to measure their error against. Chapter 4 gives that error at the two LiDAR KHG, 0.47 to 0.64 m over 1 km for DeltaDTM and 1.66 to 2.49 m for processed DEMNAS, so block counts below should be read as a range between the two models.

### Terrain and the three elevation models

{{figure: khg_dem_maps:14.03.02 | Pulau Bengkalis: ground elevation from each elevation model on one colour scale, and their differences. Source: LiDAR terrain model, DeltaDTM and DEMNAS after the DEM correction | made by khg_dem_maps in re-peat/tools/project_report_khg.py from the KHG work folder}}

<!-- Writing prompt: Describe the terrain (dome, river levees, coast) and where the models disagree, for example along rivers, plantations or forest edges, and the likely reason (canopy left in the surface, coarse resolution, datum). -->

Both models show an elongated island running north-west to south-east, with a low band of ground across the centre and higher ground in the north-west and in the south-eastern lobe. These higher areas are the peat domes. The two models agree on this pattern and differ in detail. The difference map shows blocky patches several metres deep in the centre and the south-east, with the opposite sign in much of the north-west, and such patches follow land cover more than terrain. The ICESat-2 test of chapter 4 found DEMNAS 2.16 m above the ground at Bengkalis, with 0.21 of the canopy height left in the surface under tree cover, so canopy left in DEMNAS is the likely cause of the largest patches.

<!-- Writing prompt: Compare relief and the median and spread of the difference to LiDAR; say whether the difference is a constant offset (datum) or local error, and what that means for the slopes the later steps use. -->

Relief between the 5th and 95th percentiles is 1.8 to 11.0 m on DeltaDTM (30 m cells, 99 % cover) and 1.5 to 11.6 m on DEMNAS (8 m cells, 100 % cover), so the two agree on the overall range. Without LiDAR the table has no difference to a reference. The difference between the two models in the figure has a median of 0.05 m (DeltaDTM minus DEMNAS), with 83 % of the cells within 2 m, so it is local error more than a single datum offset. DeltaDTM is defined only for land below 10 m above mean sea level and its 95th percentile of 11.0 m lies above that, so part of the highest dome ground falls outside its scope (chapter 4). The slopes used by the later steps therefore carry the local error of each model, which chapter 4 measured at 0.47 to 0.64 m over 1 km for DeltaDTM and 1.66 to 2.49 m for processed DEMNAS.

### Sub-KHG units

{{figure: khg_units_maps:14.03.02 | Pulau Bengkalis: sub-KHG units delineated on each elevation model. Source: sub-KHG delineation on each elevation model, mapped canals | made by khg_units_maps in re-peat/tools/project_report_khg.py from the KHG work folder}}

<!-- Writing prompt: How many units each model gives, how their boundaries follow canals and rivers, and where the models draw them differently and why. -->

DeltaDTM gives 27 *sub-KHG* units and DEMNAS 30, with median areas of 29 and 26 km². Both sets cover the whole 906 km², and the maps show boundaries running along the primary and secondary canals, which are dense across the island. The largest DeltaDTM unit is 105 km² and the largest DEMNAS unit 64 km². The two sets differ most in the north-west and in the central band, where the DEMNAS surface gives more divides and so more and smaller units.

High-need area is 260 km² (9 units) on DeltaDTM and 308 km² (10 units) on DEMNAS. In the three highest-ranked sub-KHG the *sekat kanal* (canal block) step proposes 97 blocks on DeltaDTM and 147 on DEMNAS, and keeps 52 and 67 for 90 % of the water-level rise; the other 45 and 80 add little and are dropped. The selected units are S24, S26 and S22 on DeltaDTM and S28, S25 and S17 on DEMNAS. On DeltaDTM the blocks lie only in the south-east, while DEMNAS puts a north-west unit in its selection as well, so block positions change with the units selected as well as with the terrain. None of the 46 existing blocks lies on the analysed canals, so every proposed block is new.

### Summary for this KHG

<!-- Writing prompt: Three to five sentences: the main finding for rewetting in this KHG, the recommended elevation model, the proposed blocks and budget, and what is still missing (data or steps not yet run). -->

Pulau Bengkalis is a densely drained KHG with a high burned share of peat in 2026, yet its two stations show no dry-season deficit from 1 June to 15 October, so the data do not show water being supplied from outside the rain balance. On DeltaDTM, the model to use here, the plan keeps 52 canal blocks in the three highest-ranked sub-KHG, holding 1.17 million m³, for a central budget of 21 billion Rp. DEMNAS gives 67 blocks, 2.32 million m³ and 30 billion Rp, so with no LiDAR reference the result is a range of 52 to 67 blocks and 21 to 30 billion Rp. The KHG balance and the high-need area change little with the model, while the selected units and the blocks change with it. Pumping was not assessed, the order of units by balance is not usable on either model, and only 2 water-table stations exist, so heights of canals and blocks need to be surveyed before any design.
