<!-- Writing prompt: Where the KHG lies, how much of it is peat, how it ranks nationally and why, what canal network and blocks it has, and which elevation models and steps have run. -->

Sugihan-Lumpur is a *KHG* (*Kesatuan Hidrologis Gambut*, peat hydrological unit) in Sumatera Selatan. It covers 6,351 km², of which 5,424 km² is peat. It ranks 31 of 476 ranked KHG nationally (high priority) with the default weights proposed for agreement with KLH and the World Bank, and rises to 24 when only fire history is weighed. The KHG is at the 96th percentile for burned share and the 2015 fires burned 37.0 % of its peat, while it is low on this year's hotspots (59th percentile) and has burned at most 5.5 % a year since 2015 (chapter 6). The mapped network is dense, with 782 km of primary, 3,012 km of secondary and 10,437 km of tertiary canals, and the national layer records 2,972 water-management structures, 2,847 of them realised canal blocks. LiDAR covers the whole KHG and 54 SiTMAT water-table stations lie inside it, so every step of the chain has run on all three elevation models (LiDAR, DeltaDTM and DEMNAS) and a station deficit could be computed.

### Terrain and the three elevation models

{{figure: khg_dem_maps:16.02.01 | Sugihan-Lumpur: ground elevation from each elevation model on one colour scale, and their differences. Source: LiDAR terrain model, DeltaDTM and DEMNAS after the DEM correction | made by khg_dem_maps in re-peat/tools/project_report_khg.py from the KHG work folder}}

<!-- Writing prompt: Describe the terrain (dome, river levees, coast) and where the models disagree, for example along rivers, plantations or forest edges, and the likely reason (canopy left in the surface, coarse resolution, datum). -->

On the LiDAR the KHG is a low peat plain with two raised areas: a dome in the centre-north and a smaller high in the south-west, both several metres above the eastern and coastal margins. DeltaDTM reproduces this pattern almost everywhere and sits slightly higher across the whole KHG, the regular offset of about 0.9 to 1.3 m that DeltaDTM shows against LiDAR in all five KHG. DEMNAS departs strongly from both. Large blocky areas in the centre and north stand well above the LiDAR, and a striped area in the north-west lies below it. DEMNAS is more than 2 m above LiDAR over 30 % of the KHG and more than 2 m below it over 12 %. These patches follow tiles and land cover more than terrain; the benchmark found DEMNAS errors largest under tree cover and cropland at this KHG (NMAD 2.65 and 3.05 m, chapter 4), which points to canopy and crops left in the surface model after the correction.

{{table: khg_dem_stats:16.02.01 | Sugihan-Lumpur: resolution, cover and relief of each elevation model, and its difference to LiDAR. Source: LiDAR terrain model, DeltaDTM and DEMNAS after the DEM correction | made by khg_dem_stats in re-peat/tools/project_report_khg.py from the KHG work folder}}

<!-- Writing prompt: Compare relief and the median and spread of the difference to LiDAR; say whether the difference is a constant offset (datum) or local error, and what that means for the slopes the later steps use. -->

Relief between the 5th and 95th percentiles is 0.8 to 7.0 m on LiDAR, 1.4 to 8.2 m on DeltaDTM and -1.4 to 16.7 m on DEMNAS. DeltaDTM differs from LiDAR by a median of 0.87 m with an NMAD of 0.46 m, so most of its difference is a constant offset that leaves slopes largely intact. DEMNAS has a similar median difference (0.73 m) but an NMAD of 2.28 m, about five times that of DeltaDTM, so its error is local and changes the slopes that the delineation and the canal-block step depend on. Even the error of DeltaDTM over 1 km (0.47 m) is larger than the real 1 km relief of the LiDAR here (0.38 m, chapter 4), so on this flat ground both open models carry more noise than relief at the scale of a sub-KHG.

### Sub-KHG units

{{figure: khg_units_maps:16.02.01 | Sugihan-Lumpur: sub-KHG units delineated on each elevation model. Source: sub-KHG delineation on each elevation model, mapped canals | made by khg_units_maps in re-peat/tools/project_report_khg.py from the KHG work folder}}

<!-- Writing prompt: How many units each model gives, how their boundaries follow canals and rivers, and where the models draw them differently and why. -->

LiDAR and DeltaDTM each give 29 *sub-KHG* units and DEMNAS 27, with median areas of 221, 189 and 223 km². Most boundaries follow the primary and secondary canals, which make up nearly all of the outlet network here (chapter 5), so the grid of straight canal lines in the east and centre appears in all three maps. The models differ mainly in the north-west and around the central dome, where boundaries follow terrain divides between canals. DEMNAS, whose raised patches shift the divides, draws fewer and larger units (up to 481 km² against 373 km² on LiDAR).

<!-- Writing prompt: Say how far the unit sets agree (best-matching area, adjusted Rand index) and which pair differs most. -->

LiDAR and DeltaDTM share 75 % of the area in best-matching units (adjusted Rand index 0.60), LiDAR and DEMNAS 73 % (0.60), and DeltaDTM and DEMNAS 78 % (0.66). The pair that differs most is LiDAR and DEMNAS. These values are close to what the stability test of chapter 5 found for a flat surface with only the mapped canals (adjusted Rand index 0.601), so at this KHG the canal network sets most of the unit layout and the terrain adds little.

### Water balance and deficit

{{table: khg_water:16.02.01 | Sugihan-Lumpur: dry-season deficit from water-table stations and climatic balance per sub-KHG. Source: SiTMAT water-table stations, CHIRPS rain, MOD16 and WaPOR evapotranspiration | made by khg_water in re-peat/tools/project_report_khg.py from the KHG work folder}}

<!-- Writing prompt: State the deficit and its period, the months in deficit, and whether the order of units by balance is usable for each model; explain any model whose order test fails. -->

From 1 June to 9 October 2026, 51 stations give a dry-season deficit of 290 mm (285 to 296 mm for a specific yield of 0.10 to 0.20), or 1,842 million m³ over the KHG, with 5 months in deficit in that window. The station water table fell only about 11 cm while the climate removed about 307 mm, so most of the water came from outside the rain balance, through canal inflow, gates and pumping in the managed concessions (chapter 8). The deficit does not depend on the elevation model. The order of units by climatic balance is usable only with DEMNAS units, where the two evapotranspiration products agree at 0.53 against a threshold of 0.44; with LiDAR and DeltaDTM units they agree at 0.41 against 0.43. The test sits on its threshold, so the pass with DEMNAS reflects how the units are drawn and says nothing about the quality of its terrain.

{{figure: khg_water_fig:16.02.01 | Sugihan-Lumpur: climatic balance through the mean year, and the balance of each sub-KHG, by elevation model. Source: CHIRPS rain and MOD16 evapotranspiration, 2018-2025 | made by khg_water_fig in re-peat/tools/project_report_khg.py from the KHG work folder}}

<!-- Writing prompt: Name the dry months, and say whether the elevation model changes the balance (it enters only through the units). -->

The mean climatic balance is negative in July, August and September and recovers in October; February dips but stays positive. The KHG mean is 69.3 mm/month on all three unit sets and the deficit months per year are 2.42 to 2.45, so the elevation model does not change the balance of the KHG. It changes only the spread between units, from 56.5 to 94.1 mm/month on LiDAR units to 60.5 to 88.0 mm/month on DEMNAS units, because each unit set averages the same rain and evapotranspiration grids over different areas.

### Priority of sub-KHG

{{figure: khg_priority_maps:16.02.01 | Sugihan-Lumpur: need for intervention per sub-KHG, by elevation model. Source: sub-KHG prioritisation with default weights | made by khg_priority_maps in re-peat/tools/project_report_khg.py from the KHG work folder}}

<!-- Writing prompt: Where the high-need units are and what drives them (fire, canals, dryness), and whether the same places come out on top with every model. Mention that the weights are the defaults proposed for agreement with KLH and the World Bank. -->

With the default weights proposed for agreement with KLH and the World Bank, high-need units lie in the west and south-west on every model, and the east is mostly low or medium need. The top-ranked unit is in the south-west on all three models. The second and third lie in the north-west on LiDAR, while DeltaDTM places its third unit in the centre and DEMNAS its second in the centre-north. The need score combines vegetation dryness, fire, peat depth and, where it is usable, the water balance (chapter 7); the peat depth model covers only 74.7 % of this KHG, so its weight in the score still needs a decision.

{{table: khg_priority_agreement:16.02.01 | Sugihan-Lumpur: high-need area and agreement of the priority classes between elevation models. Source: sub-KHG prioritisation with default weights | made by khg_priority_agreement in re-peat/tools/project_report_khg.py from the KHG work folder}}

<!-- Writing prompt: Say how much area keeps its class across models and how strongly the scores agree; explain the largest difference. -->

High-need area is 2,175 km² (10 units) on LiDAR, 2,379 km² (10 units) on DeltaDTM and 2,057 km² (9 units) on DEMNAS. Between pairs, 57 to 65 % of the area keeps its need class, and 63 to 66 % of the high-need area in one model is also high need in the other. LiDAR and DEMNAS agree least (57 %, area-weighted Spearman 0.55), and DeltaDTM and DEMNAS most (65 %, 0.72). The differences come from the units: each model groups the same fire, vegetation and depth data into differently shaped units, and a large unit that straddles burned and unburned land can change class when its boundary moves.

### Canal blocks and pumping

{{figure: khg_blocks_maps:16.02.01 | Sugihan-Lumpur: proposed canal blocks in the three highest-ranked sub-KHG, by elevation model. Source: RE-PEAT Interventions on each elevation model, mapped canals and existing blocks | made by khg_blocks_maps in re-peat/tools/project_report_khg.py from the KHG work folder}}

<!-- Writing prompt: Where the blocks go and why there (canal slope, burned land), how many are dropped as low benefit, and how the selected units and block positions change with the model. -->

In the three highest-ranked sub-KHG the *sekat kanal* (canal block) step proposes 86 blocks on LiDAR, 117 on DeltaDTM and 226 on DEMNAS, and keeps 43, 39 and 62 of them for 90 % of the water-level rise; the other 43, 78 and 164 add little and are dropped. Blocks go on primary and secondary canals where the surface falls along the canal, and much of the canal length they hold back lies in burned land (26.9 of 68.7 km on LiDAR, 65.0 of 110.1 km on DeltaDTM, chapter 9). The selected units differ between models (S21, S03 and S02 on LiDAR; S23, S03 and S08 on DeltaDTM; S20, S06 and S04 on DEMNAS), and only the south-western unit is shared by all three plans. The dense DEMNAS layout follows local noise in its surface; chapter 9 concludes that DEMNAS block counts should not be taken as a design.

{{table: khg_interventions:16.02.01 | Sugihan-Lumpur: canal blocks, water held and budget, by elevation model. Source: RE-PEAT Interventions and sub-KHG prioritisation on each elevation model | made by khg_interventions in re-peat/tools/project_report_khg.py from the KHG work folder}}

<!-- Writing prompt: Give the blocks proposed and kept, water held against the deficit, and the central budget with its main cost item; say what drives the cost differences between models. -->

The kept blocks hold 2.19 million m³ on LiDAR, 5.06 on DeltaDTM and 5.02 on DEMNAS, against a deficit of the selected units of 196.9, 175.9 and 227.1 million m³, or 1.1 %, 2.9 % and 2.2 % of it. The central budget is 620 billion Rp on LiDAR, 407 on DeltaDTM and 271 on DEMNAS, and the blocks themselves cost under 1.2 billion Rp on every model. Revegetation of burned land is the main item on LiDAR and DeltaDTM (91 % of the central total on LiDAR, chapter 9); on DEMNAS the largest item is the estimate of missing blocks outside the analysed units. The budget therefore follows which units are selected and how much burned land they contain, and hardly depends on the number of blocks. Pumping was not assessed in this analysis.

### How the elevation model changes the result

{{table: khg_dem_effect:16.02.01 | Sugihan-Lumpur: headline outputs by elevation model and their spread. Source: RE-PEAT results on each elevation model | made by khg_dem_effect in re-peat/tools/project_report_khg.py from the KHG work folder}}

<!-- Writing prompt: Which outputs move most with the elevation model and which barely move; for this KHG, which model you would use for decisions and why. -->

The KHG balance does not move (69.3 mm/month on every model), and the number of units, the high-need area and the number of high-need units change by a factor of 1.2 or less. The outputs that depend on canal slopes move most: blocks needed by the slope rule of the ranking over the whole KHG range from 3,634 on LiDAR to 25,580 on DEMNAS (×7.0), and blocks proposed by the head rule by ×2.6. Water held and the central budget each move by ×2.3, mostly because the selected units change.

For decisions at Sugihan-Lumpur the LiDAR model should be used. It covers the whole KHG, and both open models have errors over 1 km larger than the relief here. DeltaDTM is the better fallback, since its difference to LiDAR is mostly an offset (NMAD 0.46 m), while the 2.28 m local spread of DEMNAS makes its block layout unusable as a design.

### Summary for this KHG

<!-- Writing prompt: Three to five sentences: the main finding for rewetting in this KHG, the recommended elevation model, the proposed blocks and budget, and what is still missing (data or steps not yet run). -->

Sugihan-Lumpur is a heavily drained and widely burned KHG whose concessions held the water table through the 2026 dry season with about 290 mm, or 1.8 billion m³, of water from outside the rain balance. On the LiDAR model, the one to use here, the plan keeps 43 canal blocks in the three highest-ranked sub-KHG; they hold 2.19 million m³, about 1 % of the deficit of those units, so blocking keeps water in place but cannot rewet this KHG on its own. The central budget of 620 billion Rp is mostly revegetation of burned land. The choice of elevation model changes which sub-KHG are selected and with them the blocks, water and budget, while the deficit and the KHG balance stay the same. Pumping was not assessed in this analysis, no community water-table station lies in the open land outside licences, and the peat depth layer covers only 74.7 % of the KHG.
