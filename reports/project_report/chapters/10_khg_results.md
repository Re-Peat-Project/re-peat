# 10 Results per KHG

This chapter follows each analysed *KHG* (*Kesatuan Hidrologis Gambut*, peat hydrological unit) through the whole analysis: terrain, *sub-KHG* units, water balance, priority and canal blocks. Fourteen KHG are covered: eleven of the 12 main KHG chosen with KLH, two reserve KHG (Pulau Tebing Tinggi and Sugihan-Lumpur) and the earlier test site Kampar-Gaung. Each is analysed once per elevation model that covers it: LiDAR where the analysis uses it, corrected DeltaDTM and corrected DEMNAS. Five KHG have all three models and nine have the two corrected open models, which makes 33 analyses. Every section shows the results side by side and closes with how far the choice of elevation model changes them. Chapters 4 to 9 describe the methods and the first four KHG; here the results are read KHG by KHG.

Ranks use default weights proposed for agreement with KLH and the World Bank and adjustable in the dashboard.

## 10.1 Data readiness and national rank

The KHG that rank highest nationally are not the ones with the best data. Deciding where to work first and judging where the toolkit's results are most reliable are therefore separate questions.

The KHG fall into two groups.

- Five KHG have the analysis on all three elevation models: Saleh-Sugihan, Sugihan-Lumpur, Mendahara-Batanghari, Air Hitam Laut-Buntu Kecil and the earlier test site Kampar-Gaung. All are in Sumatra and all have LiDAR, Saleh-Sugihan only for about 62 % of its area.
- Nine KHG are analysed on corrected DeltaDTM and DEMNAS only: Pungurbesar-Kapuas, Balangan-Batangalai, Kahayan-Sebangau, Mempawah-Peniti, Kapuas-Barito and Buru Mappi-Buru Obaa (Kalimantan and Papua, no LiDAR), and Pulau Bengkalis, Pulau Tebing Tinggi and Pulau Padang in Riau. Pulau Padang has a 2 m LiDAR over the whole KHG, which the analysis leaves out (chapter 3, section 3.7); Bengkalis and Tebing Tinggi have none.

The Kalimantan and Papua KHG rank high nationally (Pungurbesar-Kapuas first, Balangan-Batangalai fourth, Kahayan-Sebangau seventh), yet none has LiDAR, and Kahayan-Sebangau, Kapuas-Barito and Buru Mappi-Buru Obaa have no modelled peat depth. Their results rest on the corrected open elevation models, with the uncertainty that chapter 4 describes. Buru Mappi-Buru Obaa has 31 km of mapped canals, no structures and no stations; its national rank comes from hotspots. The KHG with the most data, Kampar-Gaung, with 92 stations and 2,799 structures, ranks 178th, while the top-ranked KHG have little terrain or water-table data.

The twelfth main KHG, Cawang-Lalang, is not yet analysed: its name does not match the official boundary file and is to be confirmed with KLH (chapter 2).

## {n} Across the KHG

Across the 14 KHG, the elevation model changes the number of canal blocks far more than any other choice. It changes the sub-KHG layout and the high-need area little, and it does not change the KHG water balance. The budget follows the treatments that the selected sub-KHG receive, and through them the elevation model. The two tables below set the main result of each step side by side, for the five KHG with LiDAR and for the nine without.

All 33 analyses use the same settings, including the KLH block spacing (at least 500 m between new blocks, 100 m merge distance). Pumping was not enabled in any of them. Five patterns stand out.

- The *sub-KHG* count is 22 to 30 per analysis, and 26 to 30 everywhere except Balangan-Batangalai (22 units on 137 km² of peat). The tertile classes give 8 to 10 high-need units; their area runs from 116 km² at Balangan-Batangalai (DeltaDTM) to 3,003 km² at Kampar-Gaung (DEMNAS). Within one KHG the elevation model changes the unit count by 0 to 3 and the high-need area by 1 to 39 %: 16 to 39 % across the three models at the five LiDAR KHG (the 39 % at Saleh-Sugihan, where LiDAR covers part of the KHG) and 1 to 23 % between DeltaDTM and DEMNAS at the nine others.
- The supply deficit from the *neraca air* (water balance) is a property of the KHG and does not depend on the elevation model. It is 348 mm at Kahayan-Sebangau (one station, so low confidence), 299 mm at Kapuas-Barito (6 stations), 290 mm at Sugihan-Lumpur (51), 211 mm at Air Hitam Laut-Buntu Kecil (8), 132 mm at Pungurbesar-Kapuas (3), 119 mm at Mendahara-Batanghari (12) and 7 mm at Kampar-Gaung (78). It is 0 mm at Pulau Padang, Pulau Bengkalis and Pulau Tebing Tinggi, where rain exceeded evapotranspiration by 153, 220 and 105 mm from 1 June to 15 October. Four KHG have no station inside them (Saleh-Sugihan, Mempawah-Peniti, Balangan-Batangalai, Buru Mappi-Buru Obaa), so no deficit can be computed there. The deficit rests on the stations that enter the calculation, which can be fewer than the stations in Table 10.1 (3 of 5 at Pungurbesar-Kapuas, 78 of 92 at Kampar-Gaung).
- The canal blocks needed by the slope rule of the ranking depend most on the elevation model. At the five LiDAR KHG the highest count is 2.6 to 7.0 times the lowest: 1,012 to 2,616 at Air Hitam Laut-Buntu Kecil (2.6), 1,675 to 9,518 at Kampar-Gaung (5.7), 1,995 to 8,763 at Mendahara-Batanghari (4.4), 1,165 to 7,752 at Saleh-Sugihan (6.7) and 3,634 to 25,580 at Sugihan-Lumpur (7.0). At the nine others DEMNAS gives 1.6 to 4.2 times the DeltaDTM count: 2,291 to 3,732 at Pulau Bengkalis (1.6), 3,276 to 7,466 at Kahayan-Sebangau (2.3), 4,586 to 18,544 at Mempawah-Peniti (4.0) and 73 to 303 at Buru Mappi-Buru Obaa (4.2). DEMNAS gives more than DeltaDTM at all 14 KHG. LiDAR gives the fewest at Kampar-Gaung, Mendahara-Batanghari and Sugihan-Lumpur; DeltaDTM gives the fewest at Air Hitam Laut-Buntu Kecil and Saleh-Sugihan, where LiDAR gives the most.
- RE-PEAT Interventions proposes far fewer blocks, because it follows the 0.40 m head rule only along the analysed canals of the selected sub-KHG and keeps the blocks that give 90 % of the canal rewetting. It proposes 18 to 282 blocks per analysis and keeps 5 to 117. The 90 % balance narrows the differences between elevation models, and their direction is not constant. Between DeltaDTM and DEMNAS the kept count differs by 1.3 to 4.3 times at the nine KHG without LiDAR, and DEMNAS keeps fewer than DeltaDTM at six of them (Kahayan-Sebangau, Kapuas-Barito, Mempawah-Peniti, Pulau Padang, Pulau Tebing Tinggi, Pungurbesar-Kapuas). At the five LiDAR KHG the kept count differs by 1.4 to 3.8 times across models.
- The central budget estimate runs from 1 billion Rp (Kampar-Gaung, LiDAR) to 620 billion Rp (Sugihan-Lumpur, LiDAR). The kept blocks themselves cost 0.09 to 2.11 billion Rp per analysis. The largest budget item is the revegetation of burned land in 14 analyses and the KLH IPG estimate of missing blocks outside the analysed sub-KHG in 19. The IPG estimate is large where the ranking counts many blocks: on DEMNAS it is 192 of 193 billion Rp at Pungurbesar-Kapuas and 157 of 158 at Mempawah-Peniti. At the nine KHG without LiDAR the budget on DEMNAS is higher than on DeltaDTM at seven, equal at one and lower at one (Buru Mappi-Buru Obaa, 15 against 75 billion Rp), by a factor of 1.0 to 5.7.

The size of the elevation-model effect differs between KHG. It is largest at Sugihan-Lumpur (7.0), Saleh-Sugihan (6.7) and Kampar-Gaung (5.7), and smallest at Pulau Bengkalis (1.6) and Kahayan-Sebangau (2.3). The mechanism shown at Sugihan-Lumpur (chapter 7, section 7.5) is that block spacing is 0.4 m divided by the canal slope, and the slope is larger on a noisier elevation model; the tables here do not separate it from the canal layout. The absolute counts follow the mapped canal network: Mempawah-Peniti has 13 km of canal per km² of peat and 4,586 to 18,544 blocks needed on 229 to 232 km² of high-need area, while Buru Mappi-Buru Obaa has 31 km of canals on 1,699 km² of peat and needs 73 to 303. At Saleh-Sugihan the LiDAR count describes the covered 62 % only and the high-need units differ between models, so its factor mixes the effect of the elevation model with a different selection.

Existing blocks matter for the comparison. Kampar-Gaung (2,754) and Sugihan-Lumpur (2,847) already have many built *sekat kanal* (canal blocks), Mendahara-Batanghari has 417, Pungurbesar-Kapuas 395, Air Hitam Laut-Buntu Kecil 201, Pulau Tebing Tinggi 168, Pulau Padang 156, Kapuas-Barito 53, Pulau Bengkalis 46 and Saleh-Sugihan 25 (none inside the area its LiDAR covers). The block layer used by the ranking holds none at Mempawah-Peniti, Kahayan-Sebangau, Balangan-Batangalai and Buru Mappi-Buru Obaa, although the national structure layer in Table 10.1 lists 264, 1,333 and 112 structures at the first three. At these KHG the blocks needed by the ranking are not set against existing blocks. Elsewhere they are, and the interventions module adds blocks only where its head rule finds a gap.

The spread across elevation models is the main uncertainty in these numbers. At the five KHG with LiDAR, read the LiDAR result first, keeping in mind that it covers 62 % of Saleh-Sugihan. At the nine KHG without LiDAR, read the block counts and the sub-KHG selected for them as a range between the corrected DeltaDTM and DEMNAS results, and check them in the field before design. The KHG water balance and the station deficit can be read without that range.
