# 1 Summary

This report sets out where Indonesia's burned area of 2015 to 2025 and its hotspots of 2026 fall with respect to business licences (concessions), the forest area (*kawasan hutan*), the villages of the Desa Mandiri Peduli Gambut programme (DMPG) and peat. It covers all land inside the 2023 village boundaries, 189.1 million ha. The figures say where fires were observed. They do not show the cause of a fire or who is responsible for it. The last chapters set out KLH's priority area for peat restoration, who covers which part of it and the roles of the ministries.

- VIIRS recorded 572,701 hotspots on land from 1 January to 31 October 2026, 3.2 times the mean of 2015 to 2025 over the same months and the second highest since 2012, after 2015 (765,124). MODIS, the series KLH reports, recorded 199,069 to 29 October 2026.
- 150,715 of the 2026 hotspots (26%) and 1.53 of the 7.75 million ha burned in 2015 to 2025 (20%) lay inside concessions; most fire was outside them.
- The forest area holds 58% of the burned area and 64% of the 2026 hotspots.
- 181,229 of the 2026 hotspots (32%) and 1.96 million ha of the burned area (25%) lay on peat.
- 154,575 of the 2026 hotspots (27%) lay in DMPG villages. The villages lie on the most fire-prone peat. Within their provinces, DMPG village peat had 2.3 to 3.0 times the hotspot density of other peat in every fire year since 2015 (2.5 times in 2015, before the first villages joined in 2017). In 2026 it had 221.8 hotspots per 100 km² against 81.7 on other peat, and hotspots rose about as much in both (3.7 and 3.4 times the 2015 to 2025 mean) (chapter 9).
- 1.5 million ha burned in two or more different years of 2015 to 2026; 85% of it lies outside concessions (chapter 10).
- Concessions, DMPG villages and peat lie mostly in the provinces where fire is common, so this report does not set their hotspot rates against the rest of Indonesia. Rates are compared between provinces, between years, and for DMPG villages against other peat of the same provinces (chapter 16).
- Five provinces hold 69% of the 2026 hotspots.
- KLH's priority area is 3.3 million ha of peat with damaged ecosystem status (SKEG 2024) in 362 priority KHG (chapter 13). KLH assigns 78% of it to licence holders, inside their licences or in the 5 km buffer around them, 13% to the Ministry of Forestry and 9% to KLH/BPLH (chapters 14 and 15).
- The burned-area maps of 2024 and 2026 are incomplete as delivered (0.6 and 0.2 ha per VIIRS hotspot against 2.1 to 6.4 ha in the other years), so 2026 is described through its hotspots.
