# 13 The KLH priority area

KLH has set a restoration target of about 3.3 million ha in 337 priority KHG, chosen on the peat ecosystem damage status (SKEG), in particular burned and canal-affected land. In KLH's integrated thematic analysis for peat ecosystem restoration (PPEG, April 2026), peat with SKEG 2024 damage of any class covers 3.3 million ha. This report takes that peat as the priority area. A KHG is a priority KHG when it lies in one of the 2,004 target villages of the Bluebook 2026 (345 KHG) or holds damaged peat (17 more): 362 KHG. The 337 KHG of the target are not listed in the sources, so this list is a reconstruction.

![The priority KHG and the damaged peat by attribute: the reason each KHG is listed, the SKEG damage class, whether the peat lies in a target village, on a peat dome and on peat 3 m or deeper. Source: KLH PPEG integrated analysis, Bluebook 2026 target villages.](re-peat/reports/fire_landstatus/figures/fig_prio_parts.png)

311 KHG meet both conditions; 34 lie in target villages without damaged peat, and 17 hold damaged peat outside the target villages. Most damage is light (78%); 11% is moderate and 7% heavy. The other 4% lies outside any KHG, where KLH's file codes the peat as damaged but gives no damage class. 90% of the priority area lies in the target villages. 1.4 million ha (43%) lies on peat domes, the ombrogenous part, and 1.5 million ha (46%) on peat 3 m or deeper. Both come from KLH's landform and thickness classes, which are blank in places; Pulau Bengkalis, for one, has no dome in the landform field.

![The 20 KHG with the most damaged peat, on a peat dome and elsewhere. Source: KLH PPEG integrated analysis.](re-peat/reports/fire_landstatus/figures/fig_prio_khg.png)

The priority area is concentrated: 17 KHG hold half of it. The largest are Siak - Kampar (260 thousand ha), Kampar - Gaung (240 thousand ha), Rokan - Siak Kecil (180 thousand ha). Table A.12 lists the 30 KHG with the most damaged peat.
