# 15 National KHG profile and ranking

Profiles all 865 KHG from the layers in `_data` and ranks them for rewetting attention, regardless of the 12 selected KHG ([13](13-selected-khg.md)). Code: `national/profile.py`, `national/stage3.py` and `national/dashboard.py` in `repeat-prioritisation`; the registry parser and the data fetchers are in `re-peat/tools` and `repeat-common`. The result is one self-contained interactive page, `reports/national_profile/dashboard.html` in this repository, with the CSV tables behind it.

The ranking (Stages 1-2) uses fire history, hotspots and canal density, with fire counted on peat. Stage 3 adds climate, land cover, surface water and the water table for the shortlisted KHG; those are shown on the profile pages and are not in the score. It is not the full level-1 run of [03](03-prioritisation.md).

## What is computed per KHG

| Column | From | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| `u_fire_freq` | burnt-area polygons | mean annual burned share of the KHG, baseline years; scored (weight 2) |
| `u_burn_repeat` | same | share burned twice or more in the baseline; scored (1) |
| `u_hotspot` | FIRMS VIIRS S-NPP 2012-2025 | vegetation-fire detections (type 0) of nominal or high confidence per km2 per year; scored (1) |
| `u_canal_primary`, `u_canal_secondary`, `u_canal_tertiary` | OSM-derived canal layer, class from `KODE_KANAL` (Kanal Primer, Sekunder, Tersier) | km of each class per km2 of KHG; scored with weights 0.5, 0.3, 0.2, which split the single canal weight used before (my default: the larger canals drain more; change it in the config or with the sliders). A KHG with no canal in the layer gets no value (not mapped), and its other parameters carry the score. `u_canal_density` (all classes together) is reported with weight 0 |
| `u_hotspot_now` | MODIS hotspots of the current year (`hotspots_modis_2026.gpkg`: the KLH file to its last date, NASA FIRMS MODIS NRT after it) | hotspots of medium or high confidence on peat per km2 of peat, year to date; scored with its **own weight** (config `indicators.u_hotspot_now`, 0 drops it) and also ranked alone, by density (`peringkat_now`) and by number of hotspots (`peringkat_now_n`): density favours KHG with little peat, the count favours large ones, and which answers "most severe this year" is the team's call. Left out of `--mode validate`, because the current year comes after the held-out years |
| `u_burned_now` | burnt-area polygons, current-year flag (`BA_2026`) | burned share of the peat this year; reported, weight 0 |
| `u_frp`, `frp_mean_mw` | same hotspots | fire radiative power: reported, weight 0 (correlation 0.99 with hotspot density). Severity proxy |
| `n_infra_ada`, `n_desa_target` | BRGM structures, Bluebook villages | counts, reported |
| `n_dmpg`, `dmpg_km2` | DMPG villages of the SiTMAT portal (`dpg/dmpg_villages_sitmat.gpkg`, layer `dmpg_villages`, 838 villages) | villages whose representative point lies in the KHG, and the village land inside the KHG in km2 (equal-area); reported, not scored |
| `i_peat_depth`, `peat_cover_frac` | BBSDLP 1:50,000 depth map | reported, weight 0: the map covers about 100 of 865 KHG |
| `n_tma_stations`, `tma_deep_share` | SiTMAT | stations inside and share of days deeper than 0.4 m; one season (2026), reported |
| `confidence` | derived | high = canal layer present, enough fire signal (30 baseline detections or any burned area) and area of 50 km2 or more; medium = two of three; low otherwise |

## Peat basis

The rates are per km2 of **peat**, with fire counted on peat only (`peat.basis: peat` in the config). The peat area per KHG comes from the Global Forest Watch peat extent (a composite, rasterised or resampled to 30 m; CC BY 4.0), which `repeat_common.fetch_peat_extent` builds from GFW's public tile cache at about 76 m. GFW describes it as a composite: for Indonesia the union of Miettinen et al. 2016 and Gumbricht et al. 2017 (so it can exceed either alone), and the Miettinen paper covers Peninsular Malaysia, Sumatra and Borneo, so Papua rests on Gumbricht's modelled map. Cells with partial transparency count as peat (fully opaque cells only would give about 6% less peat in a 24-tile sample). The report compares the peat share with the 1 km map per island, for the tiles that touch a KHG. "On peat" is a point sample: a burned patch counts as peat if its representative point is on a peat cell, a hotspot if the cell under it is peat (a MODIS pixel is much larger than a 76 m cell). KHG with under `min_peat_km2` (25) of peat are not ranked. The same ranking on KHG area is kept as `peringkat_area` for comparison (`peat.basis: area` switches everything back).

Other peat sources checked on 2026-10-22: BIG's RBI REST services (geoservices.big.go.id/rbi) have no national peat theme; the Ministry of Agriculture 1:50,000 map (Dec 2019) is listed by Trase only as province and kabupaten aggregates; the GFW download endpoint needs a free API key (the tile cache does not); the Global Peatland Map 2.0 (1 km, CC BY-NC-SA, non-commercial) is in `_data` as a cross-check.

## Stage 3: layers for the shortlisted KHG

For the top N KHG the engine's fetcher (`repeat-national-run --fetch-only`, config `configs/examples/national_stage3.yaml`) and `repeat-national-stage3` add: the TerraClimate dry-season water deficit and monthly rain (2015-2021; the dataset ends in 2021), ESA WorldCover land-cover shares and the share of permanent open water (both sampled at 250 m), the peat extent, and SiTMAT. Results: `reports/national_profile/stage3/stage3_profile.csv` (one row per KHG) and `stage3_yearly.csv`.

- **JRC** is the European Commission's Joint Research Centre. Its Global Surface Water dataset maps, from Landsat, how many months per year each 30 m cell holds water; "permanent water" here is the share of cells with water in 10 or more months. It is shown, not scored.
- **ERA5 dry spells are not used.** The hourly store costs about 70 s per month per KHG to read, and 25 km cannot tell neighbouring KHG apart. The reader is still in `repeat_common` for other uses.
- NDMI from Sentinel-2 is off (cost).
- These layers are coarse (4 km climate, 250 m sampled land cover) and are shown, not scored.
- A bug in `repeat_common.sources.static_cloud` is fixed here: it kept only the last STAC tile, so a KHG on a tile boundary got land cover or surface water for part of its area. Tiles are now mosaicked. The old caches under `repeat-prioritisation/data_local/cache` (Kampar-Gaung `71`, Sugihan-Lumpur `16.02.01`) were built before the fix; the Kampar-Gaung JRC water layer is 67% empty, so delete its `static/water_season.tif` and `static/worldcover.tif` and re-run to rebuild them.

## Land status overlays

`repeat-national-overlay --config configs/examples/national_stage3.yaml` crosses the profiled KHG (the stage 3 selection: top 25 and the team's) with four layers: forest area function (`KWSHUTAN_AR_250K_2023`), peat ecosystem function (`FEG__Indonesia__2017`), business licences (`ALL_Updade__IZIN_USAHA`, 2025) and the Bluebook target villages. Inputs are named under `inputs:` in the config, so an updated layer is a file swap. The four layers sit in `_data/landstatus/` (licences, FEG, forest area) and `_data/desa_target_pemulihan_ekosistem_gambut.gpkg`.

- **Method:** a regular grid of points inside each KHG (100 m, or 200 m for the largest). Each point gets its peat flag (GFW extent), its class in each layer, whether it burned in 2015 to 2025, and its licences. Areas are point counts times the cell area, never `Shape_Area`.
- **Licences overlap**, so licence areas are not summed: "any licence" counts a point once, and the per-type bars can add up to more than the peat. Forest area has 21 codes, grouped into conservation, protection forest, production forest (HP, HPT), convertible production (HPK), other land use (APL) and water or undefined.
- **5 km ring:** layer `ring` splits the KHG into land in a licence, land within 5 km of a licence but in none, and land farther away; the three add up. Licences are read out to 5 km beyond the KHG, so a licence next door still draws its ring, but only the part inside the KHG is counted. Per holder, `overlay_actors.csv` adds the ring area, its peat, the peat burned in 2015 to 2025 and this year's hotspots (`ring_*`); rings of neighbouring holders overlap and are not summed. The distance is `overlay.ring_km`. The 5 km comes from the Bluebook 2026 canal-block data (field `BUFFER`, "Buffer 5 Kilometer", programme "Kolaborasi Perusahaan (Area Buffer)"). KLH asked concession holders in July 2026 to join peat rewetting "di sekitar wilayah konsesinya" without naming a distance ([Ecobiz Asia, 10 July 2026](https://ecobiz.asia/cegah-karhutla-klh-minta-perusahaan-bantu-restorasi-gambut-di-sekitar-konsesi/)). No regulation text setting a 5 km duty was found, so the page calls it a ring, not an obligation. Holders in the table are sorted by hotspots in the licence and its ring together.
- **DMPG villages and kawasan hutan:** the Desa Mandiri Peduli Gambut villages are the SiTMAT portal layer `batas-desa-dmpg` (838 villages, fields PROVINSI, KABUPATEN, KECAMATAN, DESA, PROG_DMPG, Skema_DPG; `python -m repeat_common.fetch_sitmat_layers`). The village polygons do not overlap each other (checked), but names repeat (816 distinct names for 838 villages), so the layer is only ever joined by geometry. The overlay reports `dmpg` (any DMPG village), `dmpg_prog` (programme DMPG, or DMPG together with DMPM) and `dmpg_forest` (the forest area function of the land inside DMPG villages only), each as area, peat, burned peat and hotspots, and one row per village and KHG (`overlay_dmpg_villages.csv`). Context, not scored. The earlier atlas-based layer (244 BRG atlas villages, `dpg_villages_boundaries.gpkg`) is no longer read.
- **Hotspots** of this year are matched to the same polygons; the counts by class add back to the hotspots on peat of the profile (checked for all 34 KHG).
- **Outputs** in `reports/national_profile/stage3/overlay/`: `overlay_summary.csv` (area, peat, burned peat and hotspots per KHG, layer and class; licence rows also show the area on protected peat function and on conservation or protection forest), `overlay_actors.csv` (licence holders per KHG: area, area on protected function, burned peat, hotspots), `overlay_map.json` (clipped polygons for the dashboard).
- **All 865 KHG:** `repeat-national-land --config configs/examples/national_profile.yaml` runs the same grid method for forest area function, peat function and DMPG villages without the licence, ring and map layers (`national/land.py`). It takes a KHG list with `--khg`, runs in parallel (`--workers`), caches each KHG in `reports/national_profile/land/cache/` and resumes. Outputs in `reports/national_profile/land/`: `land_summary.csv`, `land_dmpg_villages.csv` and `land_checks.csv` (class areas against the KHG area and hotspots by class against the hotspots inside, both zero for all 865 KHG on 2026-10-30). The class areas, peat, burned peat of a KHG match `overlay_summary.csv` exactly. The dashboard table shows the shares of the KHG area in conservation and protection forest, production forest, APL and DMPG villages for every KHG.
- **Fire by land tenure:** `repeat-national-tenure --config configs/examples/national_profile.yaml` (`national/tenure.py`, same options as `repeat-national-land`) gives every grid point and every VIIRS hotspot (2012 to 2026, on peat, type 0 where recorded, medium and high confidence kept apart) one tenure class by precedence: business licence (oil palm, mining, plantation forest PPHTR, forest concession PBPH), forest area without a licence (conservation, protection, production, convertible), other land. DMPG village land is a flag that can fall in any class. Outputs in `reports/national_profile/tenure/`: `tenure_area.csv` (peat and burned peat per class), `tenure_hotspots.csv` (per class, year and confidence; `ytd` marks the calendar window up to the last 2026 date, so each year is compared over the same months), `tenure_checks.csv` (class areas and hotspot counts against the KHG totals, both zero) and `tenure_meta.json`. The dashboard profile shows 2026 hotspots per 100 km2 of peat against the 2015 to 2025 mean for each class, with a DMPG split. The comparison is an association and does not show the cause of a fire. Two follow-ups run for the KHG that have the inputs. `repeat-national-tenure-dome` (`national/tenure_dome.py`) adds the position on the peat dome (foot, slope, crest: thirds of the KHG's peat by height above the nearest river, from `04_prioritisation/licence_edge/cells.csv.gz`, so it needs the licence-edge run first) and writes `tenure_dome.csv`. `repeat-national-tenure-water` (`national/tenure_water.py`) gives each cell of the groundwater model's dry-season depth rasters a tenure class and writes `tenure_water.csv`; cells held at the managed target are reported apart from open land because the run sets their depth.
- **Hypothesis tests on the 2026 fire season:** `repeat-hypotheses --config configs/examples/national_profile.yaml` (`investigations/hypotheses.py`, estimators in `hyp_stats.py`, cell panels in `hyp_panel.py`) runs eleven questions about tenure, DMPG, distance to licences and canals, dome position, spatial structure, burn history, rain, trend and timing. Each question has a prediction and a verdict rule written in the code (supports, rejects, inconclusive, or not testable when the data cannot decide); every year is compared over the same calendar window against 2015 to 2025; intervals come from a seeded bootstrap over KHG or 5 km blocks. Outputs in `reports/national_profile/hypotheses/`: `hypotheses.json` (read by the dashboard page /investigation through `repeat-dashboard/pipeline/build_hypotheses.py`), one CSV per question, `provenance.json` and the cell panels (`panel/`, `hot/`, cached by input fingerprint). The cell tests cover the 14 KHG with a licence-edge run; the tenure and DMPG tests cover the 34 profiled KHG. Evapotranspiration of 2026 is left out of the rain question because its anomalies are not plausible.
- **Reading it:** the figures say where fire falls, not who set it. A licence layer dated 2025 is matched to hotspots of 2026 and burns of 2015 to 2025, so a holder may not have held the land in earlier years. Set `overlay: {company_names: false}` in the config to publish the page without holder names.
- Not scored: the overlays do not enter the ranking.

## Burned area and hotspots by land status, all of Indonesia

`repeat-national-fire --config configs/examples/national_profile.yaml` (`national/fire_landstatus.py`) tallies burned area and hotspots by concession, forest area and DMPG village land over all land inside the 2023 village boundaries, inside and outside KHG, with peat as an added split. Every layer is rasterised on one equal-area grid (EPSG:6933, 30 m, block `fire_landstatus:` in the config) in 90 km tiles, cached in `reports/national_profile/fire_landstatus/cache/` and resumable (`--force`, `--tiles`, `--workers`, `--no-rasters`). A cell takes, at its centre: district and province (village boundaries), KHG, forest area class (overlaps: conservation, protection, production, convertible, APL, water), every licence type it lies in (oil palm, mining, PPHTR, PBPH, and PPHKM, which is not counted as a concession), DMPG village, GFW peat (3 = tile not fetched: the extent was fetched around KHG only) and the KLHK burn years 2015 to 2026. Hotspots take the classes of their cell: MODIS 2026 (headline, the series KLH reports) and VIIRS S-NPP 2012 to 2026 (trend), never added. MODIS and VIIRS NRT carry no type, so a detection within 1 km (MODIS) or 375 m (VIIRS) of a VIIRS volcano, static-source or offshore detection is flagged `static` (tested on the typed 2026 VIIRS file: precision 0.88, recall 0.67, `checks_static.csv`). Hotspots outside the village boundaries (sea, neighbouring countries: the 2026 files cover a bounding box) are left out (`hotspots_outside_villages.csv`).

`repeat-national-fire-tables` (`national/fire_tables.py`) writes the tables (`tables/t_*.csv`, each for all land and peat), `area.parquet` and `hot.parquet` (read by the dashboard page National summary, `repeat-dashboard/pipeline/build_fire_summary.py`), the checks and the review layers. Checks: burned area on the grid against the exact polygon area per tile and year (0.09% over all years, at most 0.7% in a year); every breakdown sums to the national total; KHG and forest-class areas against `land_summary.csv` (median 0.09%). `t_completeness.csv` gives burned hectares per VIIRS hotspot per year and province: 2.1 to 6.4 in 2015 to 2025 except 2024 (0.57), and 0.20 in 2026, so the burned-area maps of 2024 and 2026 are incomplete as delivered; shares of burned area use 2015 to 2025. `t_dmpg_comparable.csv` compares DMPG village peat with other peat in the provinces that hold DMPG villages, because the villages were placed on fire-prone peat. The source of the burned-area layer is KLHK; its production method is not stated.

Review layers in `fire_landstatus/layers/` (see its README): `status.vrt` (7 bands of the 30 m grid: kawasan, concession type, DMPG, peat, KHG index, number of burns, burn-year bits; about 0.6 GB of tiles), `hotspots_2026.gpkg` (every 2026 hotspot with its classes), `summary.gpkg` (province and KHG polygons with the summary attributes). Facts and figures for the report and the slides come from one script, `python tools/fire_landstatus_figs.py` (`reports/fire_landstatus/facts.json`, report figures, and `_slides/img/fire_*.png` with `_slides/build/fire_facts.json`). 2026 is read from VIIRS (375 m, one sensor since 2012, compared over the same window, 1 January to the last 2026 date); MODIS, the series KLH reports, is given beside it. The Word report is built by `python tools/fire_landstatus_report.py` into `reports/fire_landstatus/` (it runs the figure script; chapters are generated from the tables, `sources.md` lists where each number comes from, the method diagram is `diagrams/method.drawio`); finish it with `tools/docx_render.ps1`. The 5-slide deck is `node _slides/build_fire.js` (`_slides/RE-PEAT_fire_land_status.pptx`). The dashboard page has a sensor (VIIRS, MODIS) and year selector.

## This year's hotspots

The year file `hotspots_modis_2026.gpkg` is built by `python -m repeat_prioritisation.investigations.hotspot_splice --write` from two sources of the same MODIS stream: the KLH file (`_data_hotspot_Januari_September_2026.gpkg`, one layer per month, to 15 September) and NASA FIRMS MODIS NRT (`python -m repeat_common.fetch_firms_nrt`, FIRMS area API, key in the environment variable `FIRMS_MAP_KEY`, never stored in a file). The result has one schema (month, level low, medium or high, FRP, acquisition date, source). In the KLH file, January, April, August and September carry a numeric confidence, a class (Rendah, Sedang, Tinggi), FRP and the date; the other five months carry only Kepercayaan (Low, Medium, High), with no FRP and no date, so FRP is reported but never summed or scored. KLH's classes follow the numeric confidence exactly: low below 30, medium 30 to 79, high 80 or more (1.0000 agreement on the dated rows), and FIRMS detections are classed with the same limits.

The FIRMS API serves about two months, so the two sources overlap only from 1 to 15 September (`reports/investigations/hotspot_splice/hotspot_splice.md`). Over those 15 days every KLH point is also in FIRMS (same satellite, date, time, position within 0.01 degree), and FIRMS has 4.3% more detections (daily ratio 1.00 to 1.20). KLH is therefore a subset of the FIRMS stream and the join adds a step of about 4% from 16 September; months before September cannot be compared. The current UTC day is left out because it is still being filled, so the record ends on the last complete day (29 October 2026). The SiPongi feed of the SiTMAT portal holds only the last 24 hours, so it cannot fill the gap. MODIS (1 km) counts are not comparable with the VIIRS archive; that is why this is a separate indicator with a separate weight and rank, not an extra year of the history. The KLH file has 9 repeated coordinate pairs out of 167,718 (all in June and July), left in. The profile maps show the actual points of this year (filled = on peat, hollow = off peat, larger = high confidence), not cells.

## The dashboard

`dashboard.html` ranks the 865 KHG live in the browser. The sliders set the weight of eight parameters in three groups (fire history, this year, canals); the filters set the minimum peat area and the island; a switch says how to treat a KHG with no mapped canals (ignore the canal parameters, or give it the average value). The scoring in the page is the scaling of `common.scoring`: each parameter runs from 0 to 1 between the 5th and 95th percentile of the KHG that pass the filters, and the score is the weighted mean of the parameters a KHG has. At the default weights and filters the page reproduces the Python ranking; this is checked on load (`data-parity` on the body, for example `150:0` means 150 ranks compared, 0 differ).

- **Ranking:** bars show how much each group adds to the score; the arrow is the change against the default weights. `Team KHG` lists the team's selected KHG whatever their rank.
- **Rank shift:** default rank against rank at your weights, for the top 12 of each.
- **Profile:** the KHG you click. Built with `--top all`, every KHG has one; built with `--top N`, the top N at default weights and the team's. Stage 3 (rain and deficit, land cover), the land-status overlay and tenure exist only for the KHG that went through them (the top 25 and the team's so far), and their charts and panels are left out for the others. `Compare with` puts a second KHG next to the selected one in the tiles, strips, line charts, land cover and data table. Strip plots place the KHG among all ranked KHG for each parameter; a month-by-year heat map of VIIRS hotspots on peat (`repeat-national-monthly`); the line charts show burned share, VIIRS hotspots, this year's hotspots by month, TerraClimate deficit and rain; the map has burned area by number of burns, canals by class and this year's hotspot points.
- **Hover help:** the weight labels explain each parameter's source and method; the filters, KPI tiles, chart titles, legends and map layer chips have a short note. In the table, a header shows the unit and source, a cell shows the value with its unit, and the table can be searched, sorted by any column and extended to the KHG that are not ranked. In table mode the list spans the full width.
- **Hotspot confidence:** a filter switches this year's hotspot parameter between medium + high (default) and high only (KLH Tinggi). The ranking, the profile tile with its ranks, the monthly chart and the map points follow; the address keeps it as `c=h`. High-only density is the high-confidence count on peat divided by peat area, taken from `hs_now_high_share` in the profile table. The land-status bars still use medium + high.
- **Map layers and deployment:** the page is `dashboard.html` (about 5 MB: data, charts, scoring) and `khg_tiles/<khg_id>.pmtiles` in the same folder, one archive of map layers per profiled KHG (197 MB for all 865, the largest file 6.5 MB). Each map loads only the archive of the KHG it shows, so a visitor downloads one or two small files, and every file stays far below the 100 MB limit of GitHub. An archive holds burned area (one polygon per burnt-area record, coloured by number of burns), the GFW peat extent, KHG outline, canals by class, forest area function, peat function, licences, 5 km ring, target villages and DMPG villages; every feature carries its `khg_id`. `repeat-national-dashboard` writes the sources and runs `repeat-national-tiles`, which splits them by KHG and calls tippecanoe 2.79.0 in Docker (resumable: a KHG is skipped when its archive is newer than the sources; `--khg`, `--workers`, `--force`; `--combined` writes the single `khg_layers.pmtiles` for local use, too large for GitHub) (`docker build -t repeat-tippecanoe:2.79.0 repeat-prioritisation/tiles` once). No feature is dropped and tiles have no size limit; tiles cover zoom 6 to 15 (coordinates to about 0.3 m) and the map magnifies them beyond that. For display, the peat extent (traced from the 76 m raster) is rounded and simplified by 120 m, and burn polygons and canals are thinned by about 5 m; the rates sample the raster and the polygons themselves. Burnt-area polygons are drawn as delivered, so their straight edges are the source's. Serve the page and the `khg_tiles` folder over HTTP with byte-range support (`python tools/serve_dashboard.py`, GitHub Pages, S3, nginx); opened from disk the page works without the map layers.
- **Layer selector:** both maps have checkbox chips to switch layers off. The national map toggles the score fill, the team outline and the place names. The profile map toggles the peat extent (the GFW cells the rates are divided by), burned area, the three canal classes and this year's hotspots, and has the land-status switch below.
- **Land status:** on the profile map a switch shows forest area function, peat function or licences (hover gives the class or the holder) and a chip draws the target villages. Under the strip plots, stacked bars compare how the peat, the burned peat and this year's hotspots split over each layer, followed by the licence holders with the most hotspots in their area.
- **Link state:** the address holds the weights, filters, view, theme, language (`l=id`) and selected KHG, so a view can be shared.
- **Language:** English and Bahasa Indonesia, switched with the EN / ID buttons in the header; the first visit follows the browser language. Interface text, tooltips, table headers and map labels switch together, and numbers use the local format. The Indonesian strings are in `national/assets/i18n_id.js`, a dictionary keyed by the English text; a string missing from it shows in English, so a new label needs one added line. The page also has a table view with a CSV download.

- **Basemap:** both maps use OpenFreeMap vector tiles (Positron style, OpenMapTiles data, OpenStreetMap contributors), drawn with MapLibre GL JS, muted so the data stays in front, with a dark variant for the dark theme. The tiles load from the internet; offline, the page still works and the maps show the KHG without a basemap. D3 and MapLibre are copied into `national/assets`, so nothing else is fetched.

Ties at the top of a single parameter (many KHG at the 95th percentile cap) are ordered by the unclipped values.

## Two modes

- `--mode validate`: indicators from the baseline (2015-2022), tested on 2023-2025 (AUC, share of later fire captured by the top 20%), next to the same measures for "where it burned before" alone.
- `--mode operational`: the baseline runs to the end of the validation years; no held-out test. The report ranks on this.

## Decisions that shaped the result

- Hotspot confidence: low (`l`) dropped, non-vegetation-fire types dropped; both are config settings, and the raw layer keeps everything.
- Hotspots come from VIIRS S-NPP only (the one sensor with a country archive back to 2012). The 2026 MODIS hotspots in `_data` are not mixed in: a different sensor with different counts.
- Weights are provisional. Fire frequency counts double; change them in `configs/examples/national_profile.yaml`.
- The canal layer is OSM-derived and patchy: 517 KHG have none. Zero would read as "no canals" and pull the score down, so it stays missing.

## Not in the score yet

Peat depth (the BBSDLP map covers about 100 KHG), NDMI, concessions, tidal influence, fire severity (dNBR), feasibility (distance to water, DTM), and any scoring of the Stage 3 layers (they are shown only).
